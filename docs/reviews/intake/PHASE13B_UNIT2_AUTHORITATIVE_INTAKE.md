## Key authoritative distinctions to preserve

- abundant ≠ renewable (renewability = rate of use vs rate of formation)
- renewable ≠ always available everywhere (location/conditions dependence)
- nonrenewable may be abundant now but replenishes too slowly
- every resource has benefits AND limitations
- extraction/use can affect land, water, air, habitats (source-supported)
- technologies can reduce some impacts
- different resources suit different circumstances

## Terminology boundary (do NOT overteach source vocabulary)

Source uses `mechanical energy`, `chemical energy`, `thermal energy`, `fission`, `charged particles`, `greenhouse gas`. Custom course continues Grade-4 vocabulary: stored energy, energy of motion, heat, light, sound, electric current, transfer, transformation. Preserve science accurately using simpler terminology. Do NOT require: kinetic, potential, mechanical energy, radiant, momentum, impulse, electron, compression wave, detailed fission mechanism.

## Content boundary (private conservative school)

Neutral, evidence-based. No climate-change/global-warming instruction; no carbon-footprint/tax/cap-and-trade/activism/policy/community-campaign content; no renewable=good/nonrenewable=bad or fossil/nuclear advocacy. Source "carbon dioxide" and "greenhouse gas" mentions are preserved only for source fidelity where needed (air-quality context) and are NOT expanded into climate instruction.
