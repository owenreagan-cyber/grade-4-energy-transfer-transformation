# Executive Summary

The colleague package is a **tight, teacher-facing, read-aloud-driven** implementation of the same CKSci Unit 1. Its greatest strengths are **teacher usability**, not instructional content: a one-page unit overview + pacing calendar, a named 3‑5‑17‑8‑2 daily routine, a **scripted Demonstration Guide** ("Materials / Steps / Explain it (say this) / Ask–Listen for / If students ask"), a **Graded Work Plan**, and an **assessment alignment map with per-item "Test Q" tags** on every notes page and demo.

Our package is stronger on **instructional depth and grading rigor**: multi-page guided notes with models, drawing space, source→path→receiver framing, and claim‑evidence scaffolds; a far richer lab spine (Energy Detectives, Moving Marbles, Ramp It Up, Drop It, four transfer investigations, Crash Lab with isolated-variable design, engineering capstone); custom worksheets/checkpoints with many scorable items; a 25‑question/50‑point all-matching+MC assessment (binary-gradable); a study guide and clickable review game; and per-day absent recovery and substitution notes.

**The strongest import opportunities are teacher-facing packaging, not pedagogy:** (1) a compact 6‑page "Unit 1 at a glance" front section, (2) a scripted Demonstration Guide in the colleague's exact structure, (3) reader page-references and sentence starters in guided notes, (4) the Pom‑Pom Popper as an *optional* Friday make-and-take. **We should NOT import** the AP-graded-work philosophy, the 15‑question variable-point assessment, or three content-boundary over-reaches (mass/weight as core, "proof" language, long-distance sound).

**Bottom line:** TARGETED HYBRID IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDED — adopt the colleague's teacher-facing packaging, keep our pedagogy and grading model.

# Best Ideas Worth Importing

1. **Scripted Demonstration Guide structure** — "Materials / Steps / Explain it (say this) / Ask–Listen for / If students ask." Seven demos, all teacher-ready. *Highest-value import.*
2. **Compact front section** (overview+pacing / daily routine / graded work plan / lab+review+make-and-take / assessment alignment map / materials) as a 6-page "Unit 1 at a glance" packet.
3. **Assessment-alignment tags** — the idea of tagging every notes item and demo with the assessment question(s) it prepares (re-mapped to *our* 25 questions, not the CK 15).
4. **Per-section Reader page references** in guided notes.
5. **Sentence starters on every "Explain It"** prompt (colleague: "Energy is …", "The apple has stored energy because …").
6. **Pom-Pom Popper make-and-take** (optional Friday, low-prep, reviews stored→motion transformation and test/improve).
7. **One-page chapter badge + Big Question header** visual treatment for notes.

# Things We Should Not Import

1. **AP-as-graded-backbone** — AP 2.2 and AP 9.1 are 2–3 items each; one error is disproportionately damaging, and difficulty is uneven. Our custom worksheets/checkpoints are the right graded model.
2. **15-question / 50-point variable-weight assessment** — the 6-point Q2 and rubric-scored drawing/design items are exactly the reliability problems our 25×2-point binary assessment was built to avoid.
3. **3-week compression** — drops our lab richness (Detectives, Drop It, four transfer investigations, engineering capstone) and Friday FLEX.
4. **"proof / prove" language** — "Changes are evidence, or *proof*, of energy" and "evidence … helps *prove* or disprove" overstate certainty; our "evidence supports (≠ proof)" is more accurate.
5. **Mass/weight collision conclusion as core** — colleague tests "heavier car transfers more energy" (CK Q11); our core deliberately isolates SAME-object speed and keeps mass/weight an *optional* extension.
6. **Long-distance sound claim** — colleague teaches/tests "sound travels short and long distances," which their own footnote admits the Reader does not support.
7. **CK-assessment-specific scaffolding** — the orange tags point to the *original CK* Q1–15; copied verbatim they would mismatch our 25-question assessment.

# Teacher Plan Recommendation

**ADOPT a compact 6-page front section**, then keep our detailed documents as authoritative back-matter. Concretely:

- Page 1 — Unit Overview & Pacing (one-page, our 4-week Wk1–4 map distilled).
- Page 2 — Daily Lesson Routine (print a card for both the read-aloud chapter day **and** our Thursday-lab / Friday-FLEX days).
- Page 3 — Graded Work Plan (our custom worksheets/checkpoints + assessment, **not** APs).
- Page 4 — Lab / Review / Extension Plan (our 8-lab backbone + study guide + review game).
- Page 5 — Assessment Alignment Map (our 25 questions → notes/activities).
- Page 6 — Materials / Prep Overview (pointers to `MASTER_SUPPLY_LIST` + `TEACHER_PREP_GUIDE`).

This would **materially improve** our teacher experience: the single biggest friction in our current package is that a teacher must open 4–5 files before the first lesson. A one-glance front section solves that without touching our richer detail docs.

# Student Notes Recommendation

**HYBRIDIZE — keep our multi-page format, import four visual/usability elements:**

1. Chapter badge (e.g., a circled "Ch 1") in the header.
2. Per-section **Reader page numbers** next to each section heading (currently only a chapter-wide range).
3. **Sentence starters** on every Explain It / claim prompt.
4. Confirm the Big Question banner is visually distinct on every page (it already is on page 1; ensure continuity).

**Where ONE PAGE is better:** short, single-focus chapters (Ch2 Moving Objects, Ch5 Collisions) read cleanly on one page.

**Where OUR MULTI-PAGE is instructionally necessary:** Ch4 Energy Transfer (four transfer modes + source→path→receiver + transfer-vs-transformation) and Ch6 Transformations/Engineering (input/output + criteria/constraints + engineering cycle) cannot fit one page without collapsing the writing/drawing space that supports our assessment's visual questions (Q12, Q13, Q20, Q22) and claim/evidence items. Do not compress these.

# Slides Recommendation

**KEEP OURS.** Our decks have materially stronger visuals (models, arrows, infographics, embedded video) and a prediction-before-reveal instructional sequence. The colleague's one clear idea worth borrowing is **explicit notes-sync** — each fill-in slide mirrors a notes blank and is followed by an answer-reveal. Ours already has 3 "notes cue" slides per chapter; a **light, optional** enhancement is to increase cue-slide fidelity where a notes blank has no matching slide, *without* reducing diagram quality or adding slide count pressure.

# Demonstration Guide Recommendation

**ADOPT the structure for all major Unit 1 demonstrations.** Our teacher-notes already contain the substance ("say this" phrasing, "do NOT say kinetic," what to listen for). Currently that substance is scattered across `curriculum/chapter-0X/teacher/*-daily-lessons.md` and `*-teacher-notes.md`. Consolidating into a single **"Unit 1 Demonstration Guide"** using the colleague's Materials / Steps / Explain it (say this) / Ask–Listen for / If students ask format is the highest-leverage teacher-facing improvement identified in this audit. Every major demo (sharpener, marble collisions, ramp+blocks, salt-on-balloon-drum, toy-car collisions, wind-up timer, flashlight, plus Newton's cradle) should get this treatment.
