## 5. Instructional purpose

Scaffold students to hold the chapter's two core ideas — **energy can change form (transformation), and engineers design devices that use energy transformations to solve problems** — via the INPUT → CHANGE IN FORM → OUTPUT model and the DESIGN → BUILD → TEST → REFINE engineering cycle. This chapter formally teaches the transfer/transformation/BOTH distinction.

## 7. Required content (locked anchors — mirror verbatim)

- **Big Question (BQ):** "How do energy transformations help people?" (supporting: "How can I design a device that transforms energy to solve a problem?").
- **Vocabulary groups:**
  - **KEY WORD — V1:** `convert` (Reader p.32) · `conversion` (Reader p.32) · `energy transformation` (Reader p.32).
  - **KEY WORD — V2:** `engineering design` (Reader p.36).
- **Note sections (N-anchors):**
  - **N1 — Energy Can Change Form (the formal home of the distinction):**
    - **Transfer:** energy moves from object to object or place to place.
    - **Transformation:** energy changes form.
    - **BOTH:** both can happen in the same event.
  - **N2 — Systems Do Transfer AND Transformation:** INPUT → CHANGE IN FORM → OUTPUT / OBSERVABLE CHANGE (source-faithful example, e.g., flashlight: stored energy → light; energy also moves through the wires).
  - **N3 — Engineering Design:** problem → criteria → constraints → design; DESIGN → BUILD → TEST → REFINE; test results (including failures) are useful evidence; evidence **supports** (never "proves").
- **Closing:** `CHECK YOUR THINKING` → `BQ · NOW I THINK`.

Section names must match the final-assessment traceability: `p.1 Key Words` / `Key Idea` · `p.3 Problem/Criteria/Constraints` · `Engineering Cycle`.

## 8. Explicit exclusions (locked boundaries)

- **NO** kinetic/potential/mechanical/radiant/chemical (required) · joules · power · efficiency · voltage/current calcs · electron flow · momentum · force calcs.
- **NO** solar-oven default as the required build; **NO** wall electricity, open flame, hot plate, real-door hardware.
- **NO** "failed prototype = bad engineering" framing (failure = data).
- **NO** "evidence proves" language (use "supports"/"shows").
- **NO** answer leakage; **NO** criteria/constraints conflation (criteria = what it must do; constraints = limits).
- **Do NOT teach the formal transfer/transformation/BOTH distinction in Chapters 4–5** — Chapter 6 is its home (this is an instruction to downstream builders, not a Ch6 exclusion).

## Cross-cutting rules (apply to this and every Unit 1 Guided Notes prompt)

### A. Obsolete INB slides are not authority
The existing Ch6 deck may contain obsolete INB anchors/directions. Use it only as **sequence evidence, existing-content evidence, and visual reference.** Derive the correct structure first from (1) Reader/TG, (2) accepted pacing, (3) current notes architecture, (4) chapter instructional sequence — then document required slide changes later. Do NOT force the notes to mimic INB-era slide structure.

### B. Necessary-writing audit
For each blank/written response, answer: **Why must the student write this?** Acceptable reasons: reinforces a core concept · records data · completes a meaningful model · supports retrieval · captures evidence · preserves synthesis · supports engineering thinking · prepares for a later required response. Weak: "because the Reader says it." Print a fact if it does not benefit from transcription. Maintain **~15–30% student-completed content**; avoid over-blanking.

### C. Core vs supporting vocabulary
Use **KEY WORD** for required vocabulary and (only where needed) **WORDS YOU MAY SEE** for supporting terms. Do not imply every term is test vocabulary. Status follows source/pacing/assessment alignment.

### D. 30-minute writing-load audit
Before approval, confirm all required writing fits the chapter's 30-minute block(s). Mark sections: `REQUIRED_WRITE` · `TEACHER_MODEL` · `DISCUSS_ONLY` · `OPTIONAL_WRITE` · `JOURNAL_OPTIONAL`. The packet must support pacing, not slow it down.
