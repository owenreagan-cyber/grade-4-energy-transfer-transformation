# Unit 1 Chapters 4–7 — Read-Only Source Preflight

Status: **READ-ONLY**. No classroom product is built for Chapters 4–7. This file
records, for each chapter, what the authoritative Reader/TG support, so shared
factory/product risks are discovered before production.

## Chapter 4 — Energy Transfer

- **Title:** Energy Transfer (Reader Chapter 4)
- **Big Question:** What evidence shows that energy transfers from place to place?
- **Reader pages:** Chapter 4 (transfer, contact vs distance, light/sound/heat/electricity)
- **TG lessons:** Lesson 6 Sound Transfers Energy (p. 46), Lesson 7 Energy Transfer
  (p. 51), Lesson 8 Investigating Energy Transfer (p. 56), Lesson 9 Evidence of
  Energy Transfer (p. 60)
- **Vocabulary (required):** energy transfer (n. movement of energy from one
  object to another or from one place to another); sound; transfer
- **Required facts:** energy transfers when objects touch; all forms (light,
  heat, electricity, sound) are ways energy moves; light can transfer across
  space without contact; sound pushes air.
- **Measured vs inferred:** transfer is evidenced by a change in a distant
  object; students do NOT quantify energy.
- **Misconceptions:** "the distant object made its own energy"; "energy
  disappeared".
- **Demo/lab:** sound cup-drum; light shadow; heat/electrical stations (TG L6–L8).
- **Prompt risks:** "use evidence" / "explain why" without naming the observed
  change at the distant object.
- **Friday risk:** transfer stations split across days — do not make a station
  the first exposure to a required form on Friday.
- **Factory gaps:** transfer-vs-transformation wording must stay distinct
  (Chapters 4 vs 6).

## Chapter 5 — Collisions Transfer Energy

- **Title:** Collisions Transfer Energy (Reader Chapter 5)
- **Big Question:** How is energy involved in collisions?
- **TG lessons:** Lesson 10 Collisions Transfer Energy (p. 65), Lesson 11
  Investigating Collisions (p. 72)
- **Vocabulary (required):** collide (v. to come together with impact);
  collision (n. an instance of colliding)
- **Required facts:** a collision is two objects making contact; changes in
  speed, direction, shape, sound, or sparks are collision evidence.
- **Measured vs inferred:** observable changes; no quantitative energy.
- **Misconceptions:** "energy disappears in a collision"; "faster always means
  heavier".
- **Demo/lab:** rolling toy cars / marbles colliding (TG L10/L11).
- **Prompt risks:** collision before/after diagrams must be explicit; avoid
  "what happened" without the before/after referent.
- **Friday risk:** collision lab is required; do not move first exposure to Friday.
- **Factory gaps:** collision reasoning needs a before→during→after diagram
  visual; the "greater change" evidence chain from Ch3 must be reused.

## Chapter 6 — Energy Transformations Solve Problems

- **Title:** How Can Energy Transformations Solve Problems? (Reader Chapter 6)
- **Big Question:** How do energy transformations help people?
- **TG lessons:** Lesson 12 How Can Energy Transformations Solve Problems (p. 79),
  Lesson 13 Designing Devices to Transform Energy (p. 85)
- **Vocabulary (required):** engineer, device, transform/transformation, problem,
  solution (verify exact Reader/TG word-work lists before authoring).
- **Required facts:** engineers design devices that use energy transfers and
  transformations; a toaster changes electrical energy to heat, motion, and sound.
- **Measured vs inferred:** device input/output forms; no quantification.
- **Misconceptions:** "energy is used up"; conflating transfer (moves) with
  transformation (changes form) — the unit non-negotiable.
- **Demo/lab:** design a device to solve a problem (TG L13).
- **Prompt risks:** engineering "criteria" and "constraints" must be named, not
  implied; avoid "explain why" without the device and the problem.
- **Friday risk:** design-build iteration must not be first exposure on Friday.
- **Factory gaps:** need an engineering-criteria/constraints template; the
  transfer-vs-transformation distinction is a cross-chapter shared risk.

## Chapter 7 — Solving Problems and Designing Solutions: Thomas A. Edison

- **Title:** Solving Problems and Designing Solutions: Thomas A. Edison
  (Reader Chapter 7)
- **Big Question:** How did Thomas Edison use his knowledge of energy transfer
  and transformation to solve problems?
- **TG lesson:** (verify; the Edison biography maps to the engineering-design
  lesson(s) at the end of Part E.)
- **Vocabulary (required):** invention, design, test, solution (verify exact list).
- **Required facts:** Edison applied energy transfer/transformation to design the
  carbon-filament light bulb; he tested many designs; electrical energy transforms
  to light and heat in the filament.
- **Measured vs inferred:** historical narrative; no student measurement.
- **Misconceptions:** "Edison invented the light bulb alone/from nothing"
  (the Reader says earlier designs existed — Edison improved the filament and
  endurance).
- **Demo/lab:** none (biography + design narrative).
- **Prompt risks:** biography comprehension questions must not become reading
  tests; the engineering-iteration chain (problem → design → test → improve)
  must be explicit.
- **Friday risk:** low (synthesis); but do not make a required engineering concept
  first-exposed Friday.
- **Factory gaps:** needs a "design/test/improve" narrative visual; historical
  accuracy guard (dates/claims must come from Reader only).
