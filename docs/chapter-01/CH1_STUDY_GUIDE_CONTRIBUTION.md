## 1. Big Question

**Where can we observe evidence of energy causing change?**

- Reader p. 1 margin; TG Lesson 1.

## 3. Key concept(s)

1. Energy is the ability to **cause change**.
2. An observable **change** is **evidence** that energy was involved (we see the change, not the energy).
3. A **nonmoving object can have stored energy** (battery, stretched rubber band, raised object) that causes a change later.

## 4. Visual / diagram

- **Model chain: `ENERGY → CHANGE → EVIDENCE`** (mirrors the Guided Notes N1 model and the slideshow model-strip).
- Optional secondary model: `STORED ENERGY → RELEASE → CHANGE` (N3).

## 5. Application scenario

- **Before/after** — "A student flips a light switch; the room changes from dark to bright." The observable change (dark → bright) is evidence of energy. Reinforces observation-as-evidence, not seeing energy itself.

## 6. Sample multiple-choice question (if useful)

> A student sees a light turn on and a fan begin to spin. How do we know energy was involved?
> A. We can see energy itself moving · B. Energy is involved only when something makes a sound · C. The objects changed on their own without energy · **D. We observe the changes and use them as evidence**

## 7. "What to know" item

> **What to know:** "When something changes — moves, makes sound, lights up, heats up, or changes shape — that change is evidence that energy is at work."
