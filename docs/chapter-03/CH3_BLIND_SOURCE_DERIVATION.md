# Chapter 3 — Blind Source Derivation

Derived independently from the authoritative Reader (Chapter 3, printed pp. 13–16)
and Teacher Guide (Lesson 4 "Investigating Energy and Speed" p. 33; Lesson 5
"Energy and Speed Are Related" p. 39). This derivation was written before
consulting the Chapter 3 spec, product, or prior findings, so it is not forced to
match them. Reconciliation against the spec follows.

## REQUIRED SCIENCE

1. **Big Question:** How are energy and speed related?
2. **Speed** is a measurement of the distance an object travels over an amount
   of time (Reader p. 13; TG Lesson 4 Word Work).
3. Speed is compared for the **same object** at different speeds (TG Lesson 5
   "Know the Science": "make comparisons only between the same object moving at
   different speeds").
4. **Greater speed of the same object is related to greater energy of motion.**
   (Reader pp. 14–15: "the faster the ball is moving, the greater its motion
   energy").
5. **The evidence is a greater observed change.** (Reader p. 16: "Greater change
   is evidence of greater energy." The greater change is a farther cup/bucket
   move, a higher bounce, or a louder sound.)
6. **Objects pick up more speed on steeper ramps.** (Reader p. 15: "Objects
   rolling downhill or down a ramp pick up more speed on steeper slopes.")
7. Ramp It Up (AP 4.1) measures **ramp height** and **target (cup) movement** in
   centimeters (TG Lesson 4).

## SUPPORTED CLAIMS

- A gentle catch vs a hard catch of the same ball: the hard catch causes a
  greater change (sting) → greater energy of motion.
- The same ball rolled from a steeper ramp knocks the cup/bucket farther.
- The same ball bounced hard bounces higher and sounds louder.

## MEASURED VARIABLES (students actually measure)

- Vertical ramp height, in centimeters.
- Cup/target movement (how far the cup moves), in centimeters.

## INFERRED VARIABLES (students reason to, do NOT measure)

- The speed of the ball (compared, not measured in m/s).
- The energy of motion of the ball (compared, not quantified).

## OBSERVABLE EVIDENCE

- The cup/bucket moves farther when the same ball is faster.
- The hand stings more / a louder sound / a higher bounce.

## FAIR-TEST CONTROLS

- Same ball (do not vary mass).
- Same ramp/board and release spot.
- Let go, do not push.
- Same cup and same cup starting position.
- Same measuring method.

## VOCABULARY (required)

- **speed** (n.) — a measurement of the distance an object travels over an
  amount of time (Reader p. 13). Only new required term.

## DO-NOT-OVERTEACH

- Do not require kinetic, potential, mechanical, or radiant energy.
- Do not require a quantitative speed (m/s, km/h) or a quantitative energy value.
- Do not require mass as a science conclusion (TG: mass is out of scope at 3–5).
- Do not say ramp height IS speed.

## MISCONCEPTIONS TO PREVENT

- "Ramp height is the ball's speed" (false: height is height; speed is inferred).
- "All objects at the same speed have the same energy" (false: mass matters, but
  mass is out of scope here — stay with one object).
- "We measured a speed number" (false: speed is compared, not measured in m/s).

## ASSESSMENT BOUNDARY

- Students are NOT required to quantitatively measure speed or energy (TG Lesson 5
  "Know the Standards"). They explain the direct relationship using observed
  changes.

## STUDENT REASONING SEQUENCE

1. Same object at two speeds → which change is greater?
2. Read/observe the change (cup moves farther, higher bounce, louder sound).
3. The greater change is the evidence.
4. Steeper ramp → same object picks up more speed (source fact).
5. Faster same object → greater change → greater energy of motion.

## READING NEEDS

- Reader pp. 13–16 must be read and discussed (pp. 15–16 are not token reading).
- Page 15 supplies the steeper-ramp source fact; page 16 supplies bounce evidence.

## LAB NEEDS

- Ramp It Up (AP 4.1, Day 1) — one ball, 1/2/3 books, measured cm heights, cm
  cup movement, three trials per height, a graph, and an engineering challenge.
- Drop It (AP 4.2, Day 2) — same ball dropped from two heights into sand; crater
  size is the change. The dated calendar places this on Friday FLEX.
