# MISCONCEPTION MAP

> Ten recurring Grade 4 misconceptions, mapped to source-correct concepts, prevention, labs, quiz distractors, and slide/note reminders. Teachers should **proactively** address these (the TG explicitly directs correcting the "only moving objects have energy" misconception).

### 1. "Only moving objects have energy."
- **Correct (source):** nonmoving objects can have **stored energy** (ball held high, stretched spring, food, battery). (TG "Note to Teachers.")
- **Prevention:** always pair a moving example with a stored example; use the vocabulary deck.
- **Lab/example:** Lab 1 — battery tested **in a device** (flashlight) proving stored energy.
- **Quiz distractor:** "A battery sitting on a shelf has no energy." (false)
- **Reminder:** slide — "Stored energy = energy waiting to cause change."

### 2. "Energy is a visible substance."
- **Correct:** energy is the **ability to cause change**; we observe its **evidence**, not the energy itself.
- **Prevention:** frame everything as evidence of change.
- **Lab/example:** Lab 3 — cup displacement is evidence, not energy itself.
- **Quiz distractor:** "You can see energy with your eyes." (false)
- **Reminder:** "What changed, and what does that tell us about energy?"

### 3. "Faster always means more energy regardless of object."
- **Correct:** for the **same object**, faster → greater energy of motion. (Do not compare across different objects in core questions.)
- **Prevention:** always specify "same ball / same car."
- **Lab/example:** Lab 3 — same ball, different ramp heights.
- **Quiz distractor:** "A heavy truck moving slowly has less energy than a light marble moving fast." (confound)
- **Reminder:** "Same object, faster → more energy."

### 4. "Energy disappears after a collision."
- **Correct:** energy is **transferred** and partly changed to sound/heat; it is not destroyed.
- **Prevention:** trace where the motion energy goes (motion → other object + sound + heat).
- **Lab/example:** Lab 7 — target displacement + sound/heat observation.
- **Quiz distractor:** "After a collision the motion energy is completely gone." (false)
- **Reminder:** "Energy moves or changes form — it doesn't vanish."

### 5. "Transfer and transformation are the same."
- **Correct:** **transfer** = energy moves place-to-place/object-to-object; **transformation** = energy changes form. Some systems do both.
- **Prevention:** use the T/T/Both sort routinely.
- **Lab/example:** Lab 6 stations (transfer) vs Week 7 device survey (transformation).
- **Quiz distractor:** binary either/or when the scenario is actually **BOTH**.
- **Reminder:** "Transfer = moves. Transformation = changes form. Sometimes BOTH."

### 6. "Electricity gets used up and disappears."
- **Correct:** electric current **transfers** energy; energy is converted into light/heat/motion, not destroyed.
- **Prevention:** follow the energy through a circuit.
- **Lab/example:** Lab 6 Station C — battery circuit lighting LED/buzzer/motor.
- **Quiz distractor:** "The electricity is gone forever once the bulb lights." (false)
- **Reminder:** "Electricity transfers energy, then that energy changes form."

### 7. "Heat is a material/substance."
- **Correct:** heat is a **form of energy** (caused by movement of atoms/molecules); it can transfer between objects.
- **Prevention:** use "heat energy" phrasing and thermometer evidence, not "stuff."
- **Lab/example:** Lab 6 Station B — warm water → cooler object + thermometer.
- **Quiz distractor:** "Heat is a liquid that flows into things." (false)
- **Reminder:** "Heat is energy, not stuff."

### 8. "Stored energy means the object is currently moving."
- **Correct:** stored energy is energy available to cause change **later**, not motion now.
- **Prevention:** contrast "stored" vs "moving" with concrete objects.
- **Lab/example:** Lab 1 battery (stored) vs Lab 2 rolling marble (moving).
- **Quiz distractor:** "An object with stored energy must be moving." (false)
- **Reminder:** "Stored = waiting. Motion = moving now."

### 9. "A wrong prediction means the experiment failed."
- **Correct:** a prediction is a testable guess; a mismatch is **data**, and scientists learn from it. (TG Appendix E: unexpected results are normal.)
- **Prevention:** model "our prediction was not supported, and here's what we learned."
- **Lab/example:** any lab — debrief mismatches as findings, not failures.
- **Quiz distractor:** "If your prediction was wrong, your experiment was a failure." (false)
- **Reminder:** "Predictions are guesses — evidence decides."

### 10. "The loudest/biggest engineering result is automatically the best design."
- **Correct:** the best design is the one that best **meets criteria and respects constraints**, supported by evidence; not size/noise.
- **Prevention:** score against criteria/constraints, not spectacle; reward reasoning.
- **Lab/example:** Engineering Capstone — evidence comparison + redesign.
- **Quiz distractor:** "The design that makes the most noise always wins." (false)
- **Reminder:** "Best = meets criteria + respects constraints, backed by evidence."
