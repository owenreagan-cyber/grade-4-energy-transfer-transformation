### Teacher Guide — Lesson 3 "Moving Objects Have Energy"

- **Big Question:** *How are energy, change, and movement of objects related?*
- **Learning objective:** Create a visual model (diagram) that demonstrates an example of motion energy causing change.
- **NGSS:** DCI **PS3.B** (Conservation of Energy and Energy Transfer) + DCI **PS3.C** (Relationship Between Energy and Forces); CCC **Energy and Matter**. **No PE attached at L3** (4-PS3-1 speed→energy attaches at L4–L5 / Chapter 3).
- **Teacher demonstration (THE CORE LESSON, step 1):** roll one marble into one or more other marbles; vary the ways the rolled marble causes changes. Ask (a) what is the object in motion, (b) what is the cause and the effect. Stress the moving object has energy of motion and repeatedly caused changes when it hit one or more marbles.
- **Guided-reading supports (pp. 7–12):**
  - pp. 7–8: list other examples of motion; classify own-motion vs. set-in-motion; describe with speed + direction (SUPPORT language only).
  - p. 9: identify which motion examples cause a change; changes are evidence of energy → moving objects must have energy.
  - pp. 10–12: hammer/nail, pendulum clock, wind turbines all involve an object in motion; moving things cause a **useful** change.
- **Materials:** assorted marbles · index cards (1/student vocabulary deck).
- **Official Activity Page:** AP 3.1 "On the Move" (draw a before/after diagram of an object's motion causing a change; describe the model).

### Misconceptions (TG + `docs/planning/MISCONCEPTION_MAP.md`)

- "Only moving objects have energy" — TG Note to Teachers corrects (nonmoving objects can have stored energy, Ch.1). Ch.2's positive claim is the inverse: **moving objects DO have energy of motion**.
- "Energy is a visible substance" — we observe the change, not the energy.
- Conflating **motion** (the process) with **energy of motion** (what the moving object has).
- Over-extension into speed/mass comparison — **deferred to Ch.3** (do NOT teach here).
