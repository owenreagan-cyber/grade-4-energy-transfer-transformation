# Unit 2 Video / Explainer Scripts

> 7 scripts · 90 s – 3 min each · Grade 4 · neutral, evidence-based (no climate/advocacy framing).
> **CORE** = aligned to a taught day · **SUPPORT** = optional reinforcement · **FLEX** = Friday/enrichment.
> All videos: **PENDING GENERATION** (scripts/outlines only — do not block release).

## 1. Where Does Our Energy Come From? — CORE (Wk 5 Mon, Unit 2 launch)

- **Runtime:** ~90 s · **Chapters:** Ch1 · **Maps to:** Guided notes Ch1 p.1.

| # | Visual | Narration (Grade 4) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A kitchen: lights, stove, fridge, phone charging | "Look around — lights, heat, screens. Where does all that energy come from?" |
| 2 | Cut to a power plant, a wind turbine, a dam, a solar panel | "Some comes from burning fuels. Some comes from wind, water, and sunlight." |
| 3 | Two labeled bins: RENEWABLE / NONRENEWABLE | "We sort resources into two groups: renewable and nonrenewable." |
| 4 | Animated clock — fast restore vs. slow restore | "Renewable means it comes back about as fast as we use it. Nonrenewable does not." |
| 5 | Coal pile with a 'millions of years' tag | "Abundant does NOT mean renewable." |

- **Teacher note:** stop at 60 s to let students predict renewable vs nonrenewable examples.

## 2. Renewable vs Nonrenewable — CORE (Wk 5 Tue)

- **Runtime:** ~2 min · **Chapters:** Ch1 · **Maps to:** Guided notes Ch1 p.1–2.

| # | Visual | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sun rising; wind blowing leaves; river flowing | "The sun, the wind, and flowing water keep coming back. They are renewable." |
| 2 | Coal, oil pump, gas flame | "Coal, oil, and natural gas form over millions of years. They are nonrenewable." |
| 3 | Side-by-side: 'restored quickly' vs 'restored slowly' | "The difference is time — how fast it comes back vs. how fast we use it." |
| 4 | Snowy landscape with faint sun | "Renewable does not mean available everywhere. Some places get little sun or wind." |
| 5 | Balance scale: benefits / limitations | "Every resource has benefits and limitations." |

- **Teacher note:** end on the balance idea; no good/bad framing.

## 3. Fossil Fuel Formation — SUPPORT (Wk 5 Thu demo, optional)

- **Runtime:** ~2 min · **Chapters:** Ch2 · **Maps to:** Guided notes Ch2 p.1.

| # | Visual | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ancient swamp with plants | "Millions of years ago, plants grew in wet, swampy places." |
| 2 | Plants fall, get buried by mud | "When they died, mud and sand buried them." |
| 3 | Layers press down; heat arrows | "Heat and pressure squeezed them for millions of years." |
| 4 | Peat → coal progression | "The buried plants became peat, then coal." |
| 5 | Ocean floor: marine organisms | "Tiny sea organisms got buried and became petroleum — liquid oil." |
| 6 | Gas bubbles rising under rock | "Natural gas formed too, then rose and got trapped under rock." |

- **Teacher note:** do not require memorizing exact fossil-organism detail; focus on buried + heat + pressure + time.

## 4. Coal vs Nuclear: Different Sources, Similar Power-Plant Pathway — SUPPORT (Wk 6 Thu model)

- **Runtime:** ~2.5 min · **Chapters:** Ch3 + Ch4 · **Maps to:** Guided notes Ch3 p.2, Ch4 p.1.

| # | Visual | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coal plant and nuclear plant side by side | "These two power plants look different, but they work in a similar way." |
| 2 | Coal burned → flame → water boils | "A coal plant burns coal to make heat." |
| 3 | Nuclear core glowing → water boils | "A nuclear plant releases heat from a material called uranium." |
| 4 | Same diagram: steam → turbine → generator → electricity | "Both use the SAME pathway: heat boils water into steam, steam spins a turbine, the turbine turns a generator, and the generator makes electricity." |
| 5 | Split screen: different fuel, same steps | "Different sources of heat — the same machine pathway." |

- **Teacher note:** this is the Wk 6 SAME/DIFFERENT model anchor. Fission detail is NOT required.

## 5. How a Turbine Helps Make Electricity — SUPPORT (Wk 6 Tue)

- **Runtime:** ~90 s · **Chapters:** Ch3/4/5/6/8 · **Maps to:** the repeated electricity pathway.

| # | Visual | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A spinning pinwheel | "You have seen things spin. A turbine is a big machine that spins." |
| 2 | Steam/wind/water pushing blades | "Moving steam, wind, or water pushes the blades." |
| 3 | Shaft connected to a generator | "The spinning shaft turns a generator." |
| 4 | Generator → wires → lightbulb | "The generator makes electricity, which travels through wires." |
| 5 | One pathway, many fuels | "Coal, nuclear, wind, water, and Earth's heat can all spin a turbine." |

- **Teacher note:** useful for the water-wheel lab connection (falling water → spinning wheel).

## 6. Moving Air & Water: Wind + Hydro — SUPPORT (Wk 7 Mon/Tue)

- **Runtime:** ~2.5 min · **Chapters:** Ch5 + Ch6 · **Maps to:** Guided notes Ch5 p.1, Ch6 p.1.

| # | Visual | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wind farm on a hill | "Moving air is wind. Wind pushes the blades of a turbine." |
| 2 | Blades → generator → electricity | "The spinning blades turn a generator to make electricity." |
| 3 | Calm day — blades still | "But wind is not always blowing. Location and weather matter." |
| 4 | Dam and reservoir | "Moving water can do the same job. A dam holds water in a reservoir." |
| 5 | Water through dam → turbine | "Water flows through, spins a turbine, and makes electricity." |
| 6 | Fish ladder | "Dams can block fish, so engineers build fish ladders to help them pass." |

- **Teacher note:** set up the Water Wheel lab by ending on 'falling water → spinning wheel'.

## 7. Sunlight & Earth Heat: Solar + Geothermal — SUPPORT (Wk 8 Mon/Tue)

- **Runtime:** ~2.5 min · **Chapters:** Ch7 + Ch8 · **Maps to:** Guided notes Ch7 p.1, Ch8 p.1.

| # | Visual | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sun shining on panels | "Sunlight can make electricity. A solar cell converts sunlight to electricity." |
| 2 | Solar oven + water heater | "Sunlight can also heat water and cook food." |
| 3 | Cloudy day / night | "But the sun is not always up. Solar depends on the light that is available." |
| 4 | Underground heat diagram | "Deep under the ground, Earth is warm. This is geothermal energy." |
| 5 | Steam from a geothermal well → turbine | "Earth's heat can boil water into steam to spin a turbine." |
| 6 | Volcano region map | "Geothermal works best in special places, like near volcanoes." |

- **Teacher note:** keep both resources factual; note both depend on location and conditions.
