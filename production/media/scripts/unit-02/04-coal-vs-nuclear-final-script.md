## Core message (what students should leave knowing)

**Coal plant:** coal → heat → water becomes steam → steam spins turbine → turbine turns generator → electricity.

**Nuclear plant:** uranium/nuclear source → heat → water becomes steam → steam spins turbine → turbine turns generator → electricity.

- **SAME:** heat · steam · turbine · generator
- **DIFFERENT:** the source used to produce the heat

*(Do NOT teach detailed fission / atomic splitting.)*

## Narration master (full)

> "Coal power plants and nuclear power plants begin differently—but much of the pathway after that is surprisingly similar.
> In a coal power plant, coal is burned to produce heat. The heat makes steam. The steam spins a turbine, and the turbine turns a generator.
> In a nuclear power plant, a nuclear energy source produces heat. That heat makes steam. The steam spins a turbine, and the turbine turns a generator.
> After the heat is produced, the two systems share the same basic pathway.
> The main difference in this model is the source used to produce the heat."
