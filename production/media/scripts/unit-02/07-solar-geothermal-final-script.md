# 07 — Sunlight & Earth Heat: Solar and Geothermal Energy

> **Status:** PRODUCTION READY — OPTIONAL (script only; no video file yet)
> **Placement:** Week 8 · **Role:** SUPPORT / OPTIONAL
> **Target runtime:** 100–140 seconds · **Grade:** 4 · **Narration pace:** ~110–135 wpm
> **Purpose:** Short compare/contrast of two location-dependent renewable resources.

**Preferred placement:** Week 8 **Tuesday**, as a short compare/contrast after students already learned solar on Monday. (Do NOT force it into both Monday and Tuesday.)

---

## Core message (what students should leave knowing)

- **Solar** uses sunlight; solar cells can produce electricity; output depends on available light; works best where useful sunlight is available.
- **Geothermal** uses heat from inside Earth; can heat buildings directly; can help generate electricity in suitable places; depends on location/geology.
- **Both** are renewable resources; useful only under suitable conditions; have benefits and limitations.

---

## Scene breakdown

### SCENE 1 — TWO SOURCES (8–10 sec)
- **Visual:** split screen — the sun above on one side; an Earth cross-section on the other.
- **Narration:** "Two renewable resources come from very different places: sunlight from above us, and heat from inside Earth."

### SCENE 2 — SOLAR (25–30 sec)
- **Visual:** sunlight → solar panel → a small electrical device (light or meter) lighting up.
- **On-screen text:** `SUNLIGHT → SOLAR CELL → ELECTRICITY`
- **Narration:** "Solar cells can change light energy from the sun into electrical energy. The amount of electricity produced depends on how much useful light reaches the solar device."
- *(Do NOT mention electrons or photons.)*

### SCENE 3 — SOLAR LIMITATION (10–15 sec)
- **Visual:** three small panels — bright sunlight; partial shade; night.
- **Narration:** "Solar output can change when the amount of available light changes."
- *(Do NOT say solar completely stops during all cloudy weather.)*

### SCENE 4 — GEOTHERMAL (25–30 sec)
- **Visual:** Earth cross-section with warm underground rock; pipes running to a building; an optional small turbine/generator illustration.
- **On-screen text:** `EARTH HEAT → HEATING` · or · `EARTH HEAT → ELECTRICITY`
- **Narration:** "Geothermal systems use heat from inside Earth. That heat can warm buildings directly, and in some locations it can help produce electricity."

### SCENE 5 — LOCATION (10–15 sec)
- **Visual:** map-style or diagram showing a limited spot with accessible underground heat.
- **Narration:** "Geothermal energy works best where underground heat can be reached and used safely."

### SCENE 6 — COMPARE (15–20 sec)
- **Visual:** two-column layout:
  - `SOLAR` — `depends on available sunlight`
  - `GEOTHERMAL` — `depends on suitable underground heat`
- **Narration:** "Both are renewable, but each depends on different conditions."

### SCENE 7 — THINK (5–8 sec)
- **Visual:** sun + Earth cross-section side by side.
- **On-screen text (end card):** `What condition matters most for each resource?`
- **Narration:** *(none — hold for student discussion)*
