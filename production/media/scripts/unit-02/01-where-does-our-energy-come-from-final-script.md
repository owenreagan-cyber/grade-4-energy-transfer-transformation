# 01 — Where Does Our Energy Come From?

> **Status:** PRODUCTION READY — OPTIONAL (script only; no video file yet)
> **Placement:** Week 5 Monday · Unit 2 launch · **Role:** SUPPORT / OPTIONAL
> **Target runtime:** 75–100 seconds · **Grade:** 4 · **Narration pace:** ~110–135 wpm
> **Purpose:** Bridge Unit 1 ("energy causes change / transfers / transforms") into Unit 2 ("people use natural resources as energy sources").

---

## Core message (what students should leave knowing)

1. People get useful energy from natural resources.
2. Some resources are renewable.
3. Some resources are nonrenewable.
4. Renewable/nonrenewable describes how quickly a resource is replaced compared with how quickly people use it.
5. Different resources have different benefits and limitations.

## Scene breakdown

### SCENE 1 — HOOK (5–8 sec)
- **Visual:** quick cuts — classroom lights on; a school bus; a spinning fan; a phone/tablet charging; food cooking.
- **On-screen text:** `WHERE DOES OUR ENERGY COME FROM?`

### SCENE 2 — NATURAL RESOURCES (15–20 sec)
- **Visual:** montage — coal; oil/petroleum; natural gas; uranium; wind; moving water; sunlight; Earth heat (one label each).
- **Narration:** "Many energy systems begin with natural resources from Earth and its surroundings."

### SCENE 3 — NONRENEWABLE (15–20 sec)
- **Visual:** coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium — grouped under one heading.
- **On-screen text:** `NONRENEWABLE` / `replaced very slowly`
- **Narration:** "Some resources are nonrenewable. People use them much faster than natural processes replace them."

### SCENE 4 — RENEWABLE (15–20 sec)
- **Visual:** wind, moving water, sunlight, Earth heat — grouped under one heading.
- **On-screen text:** `RENEWABLE` / `replaced or renewed more quickly`
- **Narration:** "Other resources are renewable. Natural processes replace or continue to provide them more quickly."

### SCENE 5 — IMPORTANT DISTINCTION (10–15 sec)
- **Visual:** split — a coal pile on one side; a wind turbine on a still day on the other.
- **On-screen text:** `abundant ≠ renewable` · `renewable ≠ available everywhere`
- **Narration:** "Abundant does not mean renewable, and renewable does not mean available everywhere all the time."

### SCENE 6 — UNIT QUESTION (10–15 sec)
- **Visual:** a simple comparison grid (two columns: renewable / nonrenewable) with placeholder slots.
- **On-screen text (end card):** `How are renewable and nonrenewable resources different?`
- **Narration:** "In this unit, we will ask how each resource is used, what its benefits are, what its limitations are, and where it works well."

## Narration master (full)

> "We use energy all day long. But where does that energy come from?
> Many energy systems begin with natural resources from Earth and its surroundings.
> Some resources are nonrenewable. People use them much faster than natural processes replace them.
> Other resources are renewable. Natural processes replace or continue to provide them more quickly.
> Abundant does not mean renewable, and renewable does not mean available everywhere all the time.
> In this unit, we will ask how each resource is used, what its benefits are, what its limitations are, and where it works well."
