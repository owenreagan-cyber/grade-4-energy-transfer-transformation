# Lab Teacher Spec — Sound Transfer

- **Lab #:** Chapter 4 core investigation (Lab 6 of the 8-lab backbone)
- **Title:** Sound Transfer
- **Chapter/week:** Chapter 4 · Week 3 (Mon — sound)
- **Tag:** CORE
- **Question:** Can sound transfer energy and cause a change without direct contact?
- **Big Question:** What evidence shows that sound transfers energy from one place to another?
- **Concept:** Sound is a form of energy. When a vibrating source produces sound, the energy can move through the air and cause a target to change WITHOUT touching it. The target's movement is the observable evidence of transfer. Students observe the change and INFER the transfer (they do not "see energy").
- **Time:** ~20–25 min (setup + predictions + observations + recording)
- **Prep:** low (cups + membranes + paper targets)
- **Cleanup:** discard paper targets/membranes; wipe cups
- **Group size:** PER GROUP OF 4 (or pairs)

## Materials (per group)
- plastic cup (1)
- stretched membrane or paper square over the cup opening (1)
- tapping object: pencil or craft stick (1)
- lightweight paper target (sticky note folded into an L-shape, or thin paper strip) (1)

## Consumables
- printed `04-ch4-sound-transfer-student.docx` (1/student) · paper targets/membranes

## Safety
- No swinging the tapping object near faces; keep hands dry.
- Students must NOT bump desks or blow air — body movement creates drafts that confound the observation. Model "freeze your body" before tapping.

## Source model (locked)
```
SOUND SOURCE → ENERGY MOVES → TARGET CHANGES
```
Do NOT teach compression waves, air-particle diagrams, frequency, amplitude, or decibels. "Sound is a form of energy that comes from a vibrating object" is the ceiling.

## Prediction
- "What do you think will happen to the paper when I tap the cup? What if I tap harder? What if the paper is closer/farther?"

## Setup
1. Fold the paper target into an L-shape so the adhesive strip sticks to the desktop and the rest stands upright (perpendicular to the table).
2. Hold the cup so its open end is a few inches from the upright target — the cup must NOT touch the paper.
3. Optional membrane variant: stretch a balloon/plastic wrap over the cup opening before tapping the other end.

## Procedure
1. Predict: "What will the paper do?"
2. Tap the BOTTOM of the cup firmly with the pencil, open end aimed at (not touching) the target.
3. Observe the target. Record movement as Clear / Slight / None.
4. Optional distance comparison: repeat with the target at near / middle / farther distances. Treat as QUALITATIVE unless distances are reliably measured.
5. Record evidence: "What changed? Did anything touch the paper?"

## Evidence
- The paper moved even though nothing touched it. The movement is a CHANGE — evidence that sound energy reached the paper.

## Expected pattern
- Tapping the cup produces a sound, and the paper target moves (slightly) without contact. Stronger tap or closer target → clearer movement (qualitative). The movement of the paper is the evidence; the transfer is the inference.

## Possible unexpected results
- Paper doesn't move → tap more firmly; move target closer; still the room (drafts).
- Paper moves from a draft, not sound → shield with a barrier; "freeze" bodies; repeat.
- Movement too subtle to see → use a thinner/lighter target; darker, more visible target.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| No visible movement | tap bottom firmly; target closer (a few inches); lighter target |
| Draft confounds result | "freeze" bodies; shield with a book/barrier; close nearby windows |
| Cup touches paper | reposition so cup is a few inches away |
| Membrane won't stay | rubber band around the cup rim |

## Debrief questions
- What changed? (the paper moved)
- Did anything touch the paper? (no)
- So how did the energy get from the cup to the paper? (sound moved through the air)
- Is "the paper moved" evidence or a guess? (evidence — an observation)
