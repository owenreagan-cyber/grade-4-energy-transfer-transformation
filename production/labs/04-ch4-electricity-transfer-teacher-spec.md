# Lab Teacher Spec — Electricity Transfer

- **Lab #:** Chapter 4 core investigation (Lab 6 station, safe redesign)
- **Title:** Electricity Transfer
- **Chapter/week:** Chapter 4 · Week 5 (Day 2)
- **Tag:** CORE
- **Question:** What evidence shows that electric current can transfer energy from place to place?
- **Big Question:** What evidence shows that electricity transfers energy from place to place?
- **Concept:** A low-voltage battery powers a device (LED/bulb/buzzer/motor) through wires. The device changes (lights up, makes sound, moves) at a distance from the battery — evidence that electric current transferred energy from the battery to the device. This system shows BOTH transfer and transformation (energy moves through wires AND changes form).
- **Time:** ~25–30 min (setup + observations + classification)
- **Prep:** medium (pre-test every circuit; mark polarity)
- **Cleanup:** disconnect circuits; remove batteries for storage; coil wires
- **Group size:** PER GROUP OF 4

## Materials (per group)
- battery holder for AA/AAA/D cell (1)
- batteries (AA/AAA/D cell as the holder requires) (1)
- insulated wires / alligator-clip leads (2)
- device: LED or mini bulb + socket, buzzer, or small DC motor (1)

## Consumables
- printed `04-ch4-electricity-transfer-student.docx` (1/student) · batteries (slow drain)

## Safety (LOCKED)
- **Low-voltage only** — AA/AAA/D-cell battery holder + LED/bulb/buzzer/motor.
- **NO wall outlets, extension cords, phone chargers, or surge protectors in student hands.**
- **NO coin cells** by default (if ever used: teacher-only, enclosed holder, strict count-in/count-out, ingestion hazard documented).
- No damaged/leaking batteries.

## Source model (locked)
```
BATTERY → CIRCUIT → DEVICE → CHANGE (light / sound / motion)
```
Do NOT teach electron flow, circuit equations, current/voltage calculations, or "electrons move through the wire." "Electric current can transfer energy from place to place" is the ceiling.

## Prediction
- "What will the device do when the circuit is complete? What happens if I disconnect a wire?"

## Setup
1. Pre-test each circuit before students touch it; mark battery polarity.
2. Assemble: battery holder + two leads + device.
3. Leave one connection open so students complete the circuit themselves.

## Procedure
1. Predict the device change.
2. Complete the circuit; observe the device (light / sound / motion).
3. Break the circuit (disconnect a lead); observe what stops.
4. Record: what changed? What was the source? What was the target?
5. Classify: TRANSFER, TRANSFORMATION, or BOTH? (BOTH — energy moved through wires AND changed form to light/sound/motion.)

## Evidence
- The device changes (lights / sounds / moves) when the circuit is complete, and the change stops when the circuit is broken. The distant device's change is evidence that electric current transferred energy from the battery to the device.

## Expected pattern
- Complete circuit → device operates (change). Broken circuit → no change. The energy travels through the wires from the battery to the device.

## Possible unexpected results
- No device change → open circuit / reversed LED polarity / dead battery. Check clip contacts; flip LED; pre-test.
- Buzzer/motor noisy or jammed → check connections; secure device.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| No light/sound/motion | check alligator clips; flip LED; swap battery |
| LED reversed | flip the LED (polarity) |
| Wires loose | secure clips to bare metal |
| Device works, then stops | weak battery; swap |

## Debrief questions
- What was the source? The target?
- What changed in the device? What is the evidence energy transferred?
- Did the energy only MOVE, only CHANGE FORM, or BOTH? (BOTH)
