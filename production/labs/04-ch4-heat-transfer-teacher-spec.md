# Lab Teacher Spec — Heat Transfer

- **Lab #:** Chapter 4 core investigation (Lab 6 station, safe redesign)
- **Title:** Heat Transfer
- **Chapter/week:** Chapter 4 · Week 5 (Day 1)
- **Tag:** CORE
- **Question:** What evidence shows that energy transferred between warmer and cooler materials?
- **Big Question:** What evidence shows that heat transfers energy?
- **Concept:** When a warmer material and a cooler material are brought together, the warmer one cools and the cooler one warms. The temperature change is measurable evidence that energy (heat) transferred from the warmer material to the cooler material.
- **Time:** ~25–30 min (setup + 2 temperature readings; repeated readings if practical)
- **Prep:** medium (teacher controls warm water)
- **Cleanup:** teacher pours water down the sink; dry containers; store thermometers upright
- **Group size:** PER GROUP OF 4

## Materials (per group)
- thermometer (1; non-glass digital/strip if age-appropriate)
- warm-water container / insulated cup (1)
- cooler sample container (metal or plastic cup, 1)
- access to teacher-controlled warm water

## Consumables
- printed `04-ch4-heat-transfer-student.docx` (1/student) · warm water

## Safety (LOCKED)
- Teacher controls the water temperature — **warm, not hot** (~40 °C max). **NO hot plates, NO open flames, NO student heating.**
- Thermometers handled with care; no shaking near faces.
- No conduction/convection/radiation vocabulary required (not Grade 4 targets).

## Source model (locked)
```
WARMER MATERIAL → ENERGY TRANSFERS → COOLER MATERIAL
```
Evidence: temperature changed (one cooled, the other warmed).

## Prediction
- "What do you think will happen to the temperature of the warm water? Of the cooler object?"

## Setup
1. Teacher prepares warm water (~40 °C max) in an insulated dispenser.
2. Each group gets: thermometer, warm-water container, cooler sample container.
3. Record the initial temperature of the warm water AND the cooler object/container.

## Procedure
1. Predict the temperature changes.
2. Measure + record initial temperature of the warm water (°C) and the cooler object/container.
3. Place the cooler object/container into (or in contact with) the warm water.
4. Wait a few minutes (teacher times this), then record the later temperature of both.
5. Optional: repeat reading after a longer wait for a clearer trend.
6. Record: what changed? Which got cooler? Which got warmer?

## Evidence
- The warm water's temperature decreased and the cooler object's temperature increased. The temperature change is evidence that heat energy transferred from the warmer material to the cooler material.

## Expected pattern
- Warm water cools slightly; the cooler object warms. Energy transferred from warmer to cooler until temperatures get closer. Students record this as temperature data (real, plausible numbers — no fake precision).

## Possible unexpected results
- Temperature barely changes → start warmer; use less water; wait longer.
- Thermometer reading unstable → hold it still in the water; read at eye level.
- Water cools too fast → insulate the container.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Tiny temperature change | warmer start (≤40 °C), less water, longer wait |
| Hard to read thermometer | non-glass digital; read at eye level |
| Water spilled | tray under container; teacher handles pouring |
| Students think "cold moved" | redirect: energy transferred from warmer to cooler |

## Debrief questions
- Which got cooler? Which got warmer?
- What does a temperature change tell us? (evidence energy transferred)
- Did "cold" move, or did heat energy move? (heat energy moved from warmer to cooler)
