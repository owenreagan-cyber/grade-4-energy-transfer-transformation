## The problem
Design a **non-electrical tabletop device** that produces a clear signal when a **model door/gate/trigger** moves. The device must demonstrate **at least one energy transformation**.

## Criteria (measurable)
1. responds to the trigger (model door/gate moves → device reacts)
2. produces an observable signal (sound, motion, or visible signal)
3. demonstrates at least one energy transformation
4. works repeatedly (≥ 2 of 3 trials)
5. student can explain the energy pathway
6. uses supplied materials and stays within the tabletop test area

## Constraints (limitations)
1. only provided materials
2. no wall electricity · no open flame · no hot plate · no projectile launching
3. limited build time (one period per build/test)
4. limited material quantity
5. tabletop footprint (fits on the tray / sheet of paper)

## Materials (per-group-of-4; see supply-prep guide)
cardboard base/tray · cardboard tubes · paper cups · craft sticks · rubber bands · string · paper clips · washers · bell/metal cup · index cards/paper · tape · glue · blocks · foil (springs optional)

## Model trigger (build one per station)
A small cardboard flap or index-card "gate" hinged on a book/box. Opening the flap releases/knocks the device. Keep the same release setup every trial for comparable Test 1 vs Test 2 data.

## Test protocol (AP 13.2)
- **Test 1:** 3 trials, record trigger/output/reliability/evidence.
- **Redesign:** change ONE thing, with a reason tied to Test 1 evidence + a prediction.
- **Test 2:** 3 trials under the SAME conditions; compare Test 1 vs Test 2.

## Safety
- No open flame, hot plate, wall electricity, or projectile launching.
- Rubber bands: stretch only, never aim at a person.
- Tabletop build only; one group tests at a time at the trigger station.

## Data quality expectations
- Results are **observational** (did it trigger; what output; loud/quiet; clear/unclear), not invented.
- Failure = data. Score reasoning + evidence, not decoration.
- Transformation claims must match observable system behavior.
