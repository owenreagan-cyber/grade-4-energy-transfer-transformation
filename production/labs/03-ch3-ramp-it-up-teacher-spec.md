# Lab Teacher Spec — Ramp It Up! 2.0

- **Lab #:** Chapter 3 core investigation (Lab 3 of the 8-lab backbone)
- **Title:** Ramp It Up! 2.0
- **Chapter/week:** Chapter 3 · Week 2 (Thu trials · Fri analysis)
- **Tag:** CORE
- **Question:** How does ramp height affect the change caused by the same ball?
- **Big Question:** How are energy and speed related?
- **Concept:** For the SAME object, faster motion (from a higher ramp) is associated with greater energy of motion, shown by a greater observed change (cup displacement). Students measure the change, NOT speed or energy.
- **Time:** ~25–30 min (setup + trials + recording; analysis/graph on Friday)
- **Prep:** medium (ramp + height sets)
- **Cleanup:** collect balls/ramps/books/cups; collect pages
- **Group size:** PER GROUP OF 4

## Materials (per group)
- Identical ball (same type across all groups)
- Cardboard ramp
- Books/blocks for 3 heights (1 / 2 / 3 books)
- Lightweight paper cup (target)
- Meter stick / ruler (cm)
- Tape markers (start line + measurement reference)
- `03-ch3-ramp-it-up-student.docx` (ADAPTED from AP 4.1)

## Consumables
- printed student pages (1/student)

## Safety
- Ball rolls (never thrown/launched); keep the lane clear; hands dry.
- Cup stays in the lane; no aiming at people.

## Fair test (LOCKED)
- **WE CHANGE:** ramp height (1 / 2 / 3 books).
- **WE KEEP THE SAME:** ball · target cup · surface · starting release point (top of ramp) · target starting position · measurement method.
- **WE MEASURE:** cup displacement in cm (how far the cup moves from its start position).

## Prediction
- "At which ramp height do you think the cup will move the farthest? Why?"

## Procedure
1. Set the ramp on 1 book; place the cup at the marked start position a short distance from the ramp bottom.
2. Predict the cup movement.
3. Release the ball from the top of the ramp (no push); let it roll into the cup.
4. Measure how far the cup moved (cm) from its start position; record Trial 1.
5. Reset the cup; repeat for Trials 2 and 3.
6. Repeat Steps 1–5 for 2 books, then 3 books.
7. Calculate the average cup displacement for each height.

## Data collected
- Ramp height (books) × 3 trials of cup displacement (cm) → average (cm).

## Expected pattern
- Higher ramp height → greater average cup displacement. This is evidence that the faster-moving (higher-ramp) ball caused a greater change. The faster motion ↔ greater energy relationship is INFERRED, not measured.

## Possible unexpected results
- Ramp slips → tape the ramp base.
- Ball misses cup → realign lane; keep cup close.
- Cup tips over → use a slightly heavier cup or place against a light backstop.
- Inconsistent release → "release, don't push" + same release point each trial.
- Displacement too small/large to measure → adjust ramp length or start distance.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Ramp slipping | tape the cardboard ramp base to the floor/table |
| Ball missing cup | realign lane; shorten ramp-to-cup distance |
| Cup tipping | use a low cup or a light backstop; release gently |
| Inconsistent trials | same release point; "release, don't push" |
| Unreadable displacement | pre-mark tape at 5-cm intervals as a ruler guide |

## Data analysis / graph (Friday)
- Graph title: **"Ramp Height vs. Average Cup Displacement."**
- X-axis: Ramp Height (books). Y-axis: Average Cup Displacement (cm).
- Do NOT graph "Speed vs. Energy."

## Debrief questions
- At which ramp height did the cup move farthest?
- Did we measure the ball's speed, or the change the ball caused? (the change)
- What does the pattern suggest about speed and energy of motion? (faster → greater energy → greater change)
- Is "the faster ball had more energy" an observation or an inference? (inference)
