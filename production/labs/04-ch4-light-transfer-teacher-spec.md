# Lab Teacher Spec — Light Transfer

- **Lab #:** Chapter 4 core investigation (Lab 6 station, safe redesign)
- **Title:** Light Transfer
- **Chapter/week:** Chapter 4 · Week 4 (Day 3)
- **Tag:** CORE
- **Question:** How can light transfer energy from one place to another?
- **Big Question:** What evidence shows that light transfers energy from place to place?
- **Concept:** Light travels from a source (flashlight) to a target and causes an observable change (the target is lit/bright). Students trace SOURCE → PATH → TARGET → OBSERVED CHANGE and INFER that light transferred energy. They do NOT "see energy."
- **Time:** ~20–25 min
- **Prep:** low (flashlights + targets; pre-test batteries)
- **Cleanup:** return flashlights (remove batteries for long-term storage)
- **Group size:** PER GROUP OF 4 (or pairs)

## Materials (per group)
- flashlight with working batteries (1)
- target card / light-colored object (1)
- optional opaque barrier card (1) — for the shadow/barrier test

## Consumables
- printed `04-ch4-light-transfer-student.docx` (1/student)

## Safety
- Do NOT shine flashlights in anyone's eyes.
- No lasers, no magnifying/burning devices, no mirrors into eyes.
- Chalk dust is NOT required (messy); a teacher beam-visualization demo is optional and low-mess.

## Source model (locked)
```
SOURCE (flashlight) → PATH (beam) → TARGET (card) → OBSERVED CHANGE (lit/bright)
```
Do NOT claim students "see energy." They observe light reaching the target and infer transfer.

## Prediction
- "What will change on the target when I turn the flashlight on? What if I put a barrier between the flashlight and the target?"

## Setup
1. Place the target card on the table or wall.
2. Position the flashlight a short distance away, aimed at the target.
3. Optional: have an opaque barrier card ready for the shadow test.

## Procedure
1. Predict: "What will happen to the target?"
2. Shine the flashlight on the target; observe the change (bright lit spot).
3. Optional barrier test: hold the barrier between the flashlight and target — what happens to the spot? Remove it — what returns?
4. Trace the path on the student page: source → path → target → observed change.
5. Record evidence: "What changed? What shows that light reached the target?"

## Evidence
- The target becomes bright/lit when the beam reaches it; a barrier blocks the path and the change disappears. The lit target is evidence that light transferred energy from the flashlight to the target.

## Expected pattern
- Light travels in a straight path from the flashlight to the target; the target lights up. Blocking the path removes the change — evidence that the energy transfer follows the beam.

## Possible unexpected results
- Flashlight dead → swap battery; pre-test all.
- Beam too dim to see → darker corner; closer distance.
- Barrier not fully blocking → use an opaque card larger than the beam.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Dead flashlight | pre-test + spare batteries |
| Spot hard to see | dim the room; aim at a dark target |
| Barrier lets light around | larger opaque barrier |
| Students say "I saw energy" | redirect: "You saw light reach the target; the change is evidence of transfer." |

## Debrief questions
- What was the source? The target?
- What changed when the light reached the target?
- What happened when the barrier blocked the path? What does that tell us?
- Did we see energy, or did we see a change? (a change — then we inferred transfer)
