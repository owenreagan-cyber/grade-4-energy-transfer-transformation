## How to Play

1. **Split into teams** and open `unit1-review-game.pptx` in slideshow mode.
2. Click **START GAME** (or the board) to reach **The Case Board**.
3. A team picks a **category** and a **dollar value**, then clicks that tile.
4. The team reads the clue aloud and discusses (whole team answers — no buzzers needed).
5. Click **REVEAL ANSWER** to show the correct answer and a one-line "why."
6. Click **◀ BACK TO BOARD** to return and award points.
7. Track points and unlock one **digit** per completed case on the squad scorecard.
8. The team that fills the most digits (or scores the most points) cracks the vault.

### Board structure
- 5 categories × 5 values ($100, $200, $300, $400, $500) = **25 clues**.
- `$100`/`$200` = quick recall · `$300` = **Team Challenge** (30-second demo) · `$400` = apply · `$500` = **Team Explain** (short spoken answer).
- Every clue is multiple choice or a short spoken team answer — **no writing required**.

## Answer Key

### Case 1 — Energy Causes Change

| Value | Type | Clue | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| $100 | MC | Energy is the ability to ____. | **B — cause a change** |
| $200 | MC | A light turns on and a fan starts to spin. How do we know energy was involved? | **D — We observe the changes and use them as evidence** |
| $300 | Team | What is evidence? Give one example from class. | Something we observe that helps us explain what happened (e.g., the light turned on). |
| $400 | Team | Energy itself cannot be seen. How do scientists know it is involved? | They observe changes and use the changes as evidence. |
| $500 | MC | A battery, a stretched rubber band, and a ball held up high all have what in common? | **B — stored energy that can cause a change later** |

### Case 2 — Speed & Energy of Motion

| Value | Type | Clue | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| $100 | Team | The energy an object has while it is moving is called ____. | energy of motion |
| $200 | MC | A car drives slowly, then fast. When does it have more energy of motion? | **A — when it is moving fast** |
| $300 | Team | For the SAME object, what does faster speed mean for its energy of motion? | More energy of motion (a bigger change). |
| $400 | MC | The same ball rolls down a low ramp and a high ramp. Which gives more energy of motion at the bottom? | **A — the high ramp (ball moves faster)** |
| $500 | Team | In Ramp It Up!, you measured how far the cup moved. What did you INFER? | The same ball moving faster has more energy of motion. |

### Case 3 — Transfer or Transformation

| Value | Type | Clue | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| $100 | Team | Energy moving from place to place or object to object is called ____. | energy transfer |
| $200 | Team | Energy changing from one form to another is called ____. | energy transformation |
| $300 | MC | Warm water warms a cooler cup; the heat moves but stays heat. | **A — transfer only** |
| $400 | MC | A flashlight changes stored energy into light AND energy moves through the wires. | **D — both transfer and transformation** |
| $500 | Team | A tapped cup's sound moves a paper strip across the room. What does this show? | That sound transferred energy (the receiver changed). |

### Case 4 — Collisions

| Value | Type | Clue | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| $100 | Team | What is a collision? | Two objects coming together with impact. |
| $200 | MC | A moving marble hits a still marble; the still marble rolls away. | **C — energy of motion transferred to the still marble** |
| $300 | Team | After a crash, the cars stop. Did the energy disappear? | No — it transferred to the target and some became sound. |
| $400 | MC | A rolling car hits a target and stops; the target moves. | **B — energy of motion transferred from the car to the target** |
| $500 | Team | In Crash Lab, the SAME car from a higher ramp moved the target farther. What does this show? | A faster car has more energy of motion (a greater change). |

### Case 5 — Engineering & Edison/Latimer

| Value | Type | Clue | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| $100 | Team | The things a good design must do are called ____. | criteria |
| $200 | Team | The limits on materials, time, and safety are called ____. | constraints |
| $300 | MC | An engineer tests a design and it doesn't work well. What next? | **D — use the test result as evidence to improve and test again** |
| $400 | MC | Which sentence about Thomas Edison is true? | **D — he improved earlier light bulb designs and tested many materials** |
| $500 | Team | How did Lewis Latimer improve the light bulb? | A stronger, more durable filament (and the wire-to-filament connection). |

## 30-Minute Pacing

| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Setup + rules + split into teams | 3 min |
| Play (aim for ~2 full board passes, 12–16 clues) | 22 min |
| Vault combination + final score tally | 3 min |
| Wrap-up / celebrate | 2 min |

**Note:** the full 25-clue board can't all be played in 30 minutes — that's intentional. Teams revisit the most-used clues as review; the board is reusable across a Friday Flex block.

## Source Alignment

All clues are drawn from the Unit 1 assessment, study guide, guided notes, and Reader-aligned concepts (Chapters 1–7):

- **Case 1** → Ch.1 (energy causes change, evidence, stored energy)
- **Case 2** → Ch.2–3 (energy of motion; same object, faster = more energy of motion)
- **Case 3** → Ch.4 + Ch.6 (transfer vs. transformation vs. both)
- **Case 4** → Ch.5 (collisions transfer energy; energy does not disappear)
- **Case 5** → Ch.6–7 (criteria/constraints, design–test–improve, Edison & Latimer)

## Vocabulary Compliance

The deck and printables use only locked Grade 4 terms: `energy`, `change`, `evidence`, `stored energy`, `energy of motion`, `speed`, `transfer`, `transformation`, `collision`, `criteria`, `constraints`, `refine`, `Edison`, `Latimer`.

**Banned terms absent:** kinetic, potential, mechanical energy, chemical energy, thermal energy, radiant, momentum, force, impulse, deformation, proves (replaced with "shows" / "supports").
