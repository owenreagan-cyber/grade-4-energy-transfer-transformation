## Part A — What changed?

| Scenario | Observable change | Possible energy form |
|---|---|---|
| A light switch is flipped | room changes dark → bright | light (electrical) |
| A toy car is pushed | car changes still → moving | motion |
| A bell is rung | quiet → ringing sound | sound |
| An ice cube sits on a warm hand | ice melts (solid → liquid) | heat |
| A flashlight is turned on | bulb changes dark → glowing | light (electrical) |

## Part B — Evidence or not enough evidence?

| Statement | Evidence? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The fan blades started spinning when we plugged it in." | YES | a change happened (still → moving) |
| "The battery looks shiny." | NOT enough | looking shiny is not a change caused by energy |
| "The room got brighter when the light came on." | YES | dark → bright is a change |
| "I think the motor is strong." | NOT enough | an opinion, not an observed change |

## Part C — Stored energy

| Example | Has stored energy? | When does it cause a change? |
|---|---|---|
| battery in a drawer | yes | when placed in a device and used |
| apple held above the ground | yes | when released (it falls) |
| stretched rubber band | yes | when let go (it snaps/moves) |
| book lying flat on a table | no (no stored energy of position) | it is not raised or stretched |

## Part D — Explain (sample)
"Energy is the ability to cause change. When I saw the light bulb change from dark to bright, that change was evidence of energy. The stored energy in the battery was released and caused the change."

- Accept any response that (a) states energy = ability to cause change, (b) names an observed change, (c) links the change to energy as evidence.
