# Energy Resource Detective Chart — Grade 4 Reference (2 pages)

> Reference for the whole-unit comparison (Wk 6, Wk 8). Plain Grade-4 language. No advocacy; every resource has benefits AND limitations.

## Page 1 — Nonrenewable Resources

| Resource | Renewable / Nonrenewable | How it is used | Energy pathway | Benefit | Limitation | Works well when… |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Coal** | Nonrenewable (solid) | Burned to heat homes, cook, and make electricity | burn coal → heat water → steam → turbine → generator → electricity | Abundant and inexpensive | Mining is dangerous; burning releases substances into the air | A lot of coal is available and inexpensive |
| **Petroleum (crude oil)** | Nonrenewable (liquid) | Refined into gasoline, diesel, kerosene, plastics, and more | burn fuel → heat → move engine parts (or make electricity) | Powers vehicles; makes many products | Drilling and spills can harm land, water, and wildlife | Liquid fuel is needed for engines and products |
| **Natural gas** | Nonrenewable (gas) | Burned for heating and cooking; piped into homes | burn gas → heat | Heats homes; cooks food; burns cleaner than coal | Flammable; pipes can break and leak | Pipelines can reach homes and buildings |
| **Uranium** | Nonrenewable (mined) | Heats water in a nuclear power plant | nuclear heat → steam → turbine → generator → electricity | Reliable electricity with little air pollution | Used fuel stays dangerous a long time and must be stored safely | Reliable electricity is needed |

## Page 2 — Renewable Resources

| Resource | Renewable / Nonrenewable | How it is used | Energy pathway | Benefit | Limitation | Works well when… |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wind** | Renewable | Turns turbine blades to make electricity | moving air → blades → generator → electricity | Renewable; no fuel burned | Wind is not always blowing; needs a windy place | The wind blows steadily and strongly |
| **Hydroelectric (moving water)** | Renewable | Water through a dam spins a turbine | water in reservoir → dam → turbine → generator → electricity | Renewable; water can be stored (predictable) | Dam floods land; can block fish | There is a large, steady river |
| **Solar** | Renewable | Sunlight makes electricity or heats water and food | sunlight → solar cell → electricity | Renewable; free sunlight; no fuel burned | Depends on sunlight (location, weather, time of day) | A place gets steady, strong sunlight |
| **Geothermal (Earth's heat)** | Renewable | Heat from underground warms buildings or makes steam for electricity | Earth's heat → steam → turbine → generator → electricity | Little pollution; heat is always underground | Needs a special (volcanic) location; costly to build | There is hot rock near the surface |

### Remember
- **Abundant does NOT mean renewable.** Renewability is about how fast a resource is restored vs. how fast we use it.
- **Renewable does NOT mean available everywhere.** Location and conditions matter.
- **Every resource has benefits AND limitations.** Engineers choose resources that fit the situation.
