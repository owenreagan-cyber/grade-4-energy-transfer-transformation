# Chapter 2 Teacher Notes

> Background, key distinctions, and pitfalls for teaching "Moving Objects Have Energy."

## Source framing (verbatim intent)
- **motion** = the process of an object changing position (Reader p.7; Glossary).
- **energy of motion** = the energy an object possesses while it is moving (Reader p.9; Glossary).
- Moving objects can cause change, and "energy is the ability to cause a change," so all moving objects have energy of motion (Reader p.9).
- Source examples: basketball net (swish), skier (whoosh), raindrops on a puddle (splash), hammer/nail, pendulum clock, wind turbine.

## The recurring model (Chapter 2 slice)
```
MOVING OBJECT → CAUSES A CHANGE → EVIDENCE OF ENERGY
```
This replaces Chapter 1's `ENERGY → CHANGE → EVIDENCE` with the moving-object focus. Do not add speed/mass to this model.

## Chapter boundary (hard)
- Do NOT teach fast vs slow, ramp height, speed comparisons, "faster = more energy," mass, kinetic/potential energy, formal energy transfer, or formal collisions.
- These appear incidentally in Reader examples (hammer "heavy and fast") but are **not** the learning target. If a student raises speed, acknowledge and defer: "We'll explore speed in our next chapter."

## Observation vs. inference (teach explicitly)
- **Observation:** "The target marble moved from here to there."
- **Inference:** "The moving marble had energy of motion."
- Students must not claim they *saw* energy; they saw the change.

## Motion vs. energy of motion
- motion = a **process** (position is changing).
- energy of motion = what the moving object **has**.
- The TG Word Work explicitly models this distinction. Do not blur them.

## Guided reading supports (from TG L3)
- p.7–8: "What are other examples of motion?" Classify: objects causing their own motion vs. objects set in motion by something else.
- p.9: Revisit the class motion list — which examples cause a change? Emphasize: change = evidence of energy, so the moving object must have energy.
- p.10–12: What do the hammer/nail, pendulum clock, and wind turbine have in common? (all involve an object in motion causing a useful change.)

## Demonstration note (from TG L3)
- Roll one marble into one or more other marbles. Ask: what is the object in motion? What is the cause, what is the effect? Stress: the moving marble has energy of motion and caused a change when it hit the other marble(s).

## Do NOT
- Introduce kinetic/potential/force/velocity/acceleration/mass/momentum.
- Attach PE 4-PS3-1 to this chapter (that is L4–L5, Chapter 3).
- Reuse the old draft lab that varied speed and marble type.

## Vocabulary classification note
- **motion** and **energy of motion** are Core Vocabulary (Reader pp. 7, 9).
- **evidence** remains Language of Instruction (not Core Vocabulary) — used heavily for CER.
