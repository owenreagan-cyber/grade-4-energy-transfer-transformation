## Big Question
How are energy and speed related?

## Essential Understandings
1. Speed is a measurement of the distance an object travels over an amount of time.
2. A moving object has energy of motion.
3. For the SAME object, faster motion is associated with greater energy of motion.
4. Greater energy of motion can cause a greater change (the change is the evidence).
5. Students do NOT directly measure "energy" or "speed" in these investigations — they measure the change caused and infer the relationship.

## Student-Friendly Learning Targets
- I can tell what speed means.
- I can explain that a moving object has energy of motion.
- I can compare the SAME object moving faster and slower and tell which has more energy of motion.
- I can collect evidence from an investigation and use it to support the relationship between speed and energy.
- I can tell the difference between what I MEASURED and what I INFERRED.

## Misconceptions to Address
- "Ramp height IS speed" — clarify: ramp height is what we change; relative speed is INFERRED, not directly measured.
- "We measured energy" — clarify: we measured the change (displacement/crater); energy of motion is INFERRED from that change.
- "A heavier/faster ball always has more energy" — keep comparisons to the SAME object at different speeds; do not vary mass.
- "Energy is a visible substance" — we observe the change, not the energy.
