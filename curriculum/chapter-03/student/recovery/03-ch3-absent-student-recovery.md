# Chapter 3 Absent-Student Recovery

> Recovers the essential idea without recreating the investigation. Uses "Our class observed…" so absent students do not claim first-person observations.

## What you missed (the big idea)
For the SAME object, faster motion is associated with greater energy of motion — and greater energy of motion can cause a greater change. We do NOT directly measure speed or energy. We measure the change the object causes (how far a cup moves, how wide a crater is) and use that pattern as evidence to INFER the relationship.

## Step 1 — Read (10 min)
Read Student Reader Chapter 3 "Energy and Speed Are Related" (printed pp. 13–16). Focus on:
- What is speed? (p. 13)
- Why does a hard-thrown ball sting more than a gentle one? (p. 14)
- What happens to a bucket when the ball rolls down a steeper ramp? (p. 15)
- How does a bounced ball show energy and speed are related? (p. 16)

## Step 2 — Review the setups (diagrams)
```
RAMP IT UP!                       DROP IT
[ramp on books]  ->  [cup]        [ball] at 30 cm / 60 cm  ->  [sand tray]
 1, 2, or 3 books                    measure crater width (cm)
 measure cup movement (cm)
```
- **Ramp:** we change ramp height (1/2/3 books); we keep the ball, cup, and surface the same; we measure how far the cup moves.
- **Drop It:** we change drop height (30/60 cm); we keep the ball and sand the same; we measure crater width.

Graph title: **Ramp Height vs. Average Cup Displacement.**
- X-axis: Ramp Height (books) — 1, 2, 3.
- Y-axis: Average Cup Displacement (cm).
- Plot the three points and connect/observe the pattern.

## Step 5 — Answer (short)
1. What does **speed** mean? (Write the sentence.)
2. In the ramp data, at which height did the cup move farthest? What does this suggest about the ball's speed at that height?
3. Did our class measure the ball's speed or energy, or did we measure the change it caused? Explain.
4. What can we INFER about the relationship between speed and energy of motion?

## Step 6 — The essential model (copy into your notes)
```
SAME OBJECT → FASTER MOTION → GREATER ENERGY OF MOTION → GREATER OBSERVED CHANGE
```
We measured the change; we inferred the speed and the energy relationship.
