# Chapter 3 Custom Explainer Script

**Title:** "How Can Data Show Us a Relationship Between Speed and Energy?"
**Target runtime:** 2–3 minutes
**Tone:** Grade 4 friendly · emphasizes measured vs inferred · no advanced vocabulary

---

## 0:00–0:20 — HOOK
> *Visual: a completed ramp data table (1/2/3 books → 6 cm / 14 cm / 22 cm).*

"Look at this data. A group rolled the SAME ball down a ramp at three different heights. At 1 book, the cup moved 6 centimeters. At 3 books, it moved 22 centimeters."

## 0:20–0:50 — QUESTION
> *Visual: the question appears.*

"Here is our question: **What does this data tell us about speed and energy?** Careful — we did not measure the ball's speed. We did not measure its energy. So what DID we measure?"

## 0:50–1:20 — MEASURED vs INFERRED
> *Visual: two columns — MEASURED / INFERRED.*

"We **measured** the ramp height and the cup displacement — those are things we can see and record with a ruler. But we **inferred** two other things: that the higher ramp made the ball move faster, and that the faster ball had more energy of motion."

## 1:20–1:50 — SAME OBJECT
> *Visual: the words "SAME OBJECT" highlighted.*

"This only works because we used the SAME ball every time. If we changed the ball too, we wouldn't know what caused the difference. Same object, different speed — that's a fair comparison."

## 1:50–2:20 — THE RELATIONSHIP
> *Visual: the chain — SAME OBJECT → FASTER → GREATER ENERGY → GREATER CHANGE.*

"The pattern tells the story: a higher ramp means faster motion, and faster motion caused a greater change — the cup moved farther. A greater change is evidence of greater energy of motion."

## 2:20–2:40 — FINAL CHALLENGE
> *Visual: the graph "Ramp Height vs. Average Cup Displacement."*

"Here is your challenge: look at the graph. What would you predict for a ramp even higher than 3 books? And how do you know?"

---

## Production notes
- Keep captions on for accessibility.
- Grade 4 words only (measure, infer, data, graph, speed, energy of motion, change, same object).
- Never say "we measured the energy" or "we measured the speed."
- No kinetic/potential/velocity/mass language.
