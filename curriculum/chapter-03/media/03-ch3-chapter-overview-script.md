# Chapter 3 Custom Cinematic Overview Script

**Title:** "Why Does Faster Motion Cause a Bigger Change?"
**Target runtime:** 1–2 minutes
**Tone:** curiosity-building, Grade 4 friendly · no front-loaded conclusion · no advanced terminology

---

## 0:00–0:15 — HOOK (same object, slow vs faster)
> *Visual: the SAME ball rolled slowly at a cup — the cup nudges a little. Then the SAME ball rolled faster — the cup slides far.*

"Watch the SAME ball, twice. The first time, it rolls slowly. The cup moves just a little. The second time, the same ball rolls faster — and the cup flies! Same ball. Same cup. So what made the difference?"

## 0:15–0:35 — CURIOSITY
> *Visual: close-up of the cup's two positions, side by side.*

"The ball moved faster, and it caused a bigger change. But why? What connects how fast something moves to the change it can cause?"

## 0:35–0:55 — INTRODUCE THE RELATIONSHIP (without the full answer)
> *Visual: a question mark over the words "speed" and "energy."*

"Scientists have a word for how fast something moves: **speed**. And they have a word for the ability to cause change: **energy**. A moving object has **energy of motion**. Could faster motion mean greater energy of motion?"

## 0:55–1:20 — EVIDENCE CHALLENGE
> *Visual: the ball rolling down a low ramp, then a higher ramp, into a bucket.*

"Here's the tricky part: we can't see energy. We can't put it on a scale. So how would we ever find out? We have to look at the change the object causes."

## 1:20–1:40 — END ON A QUESTION
> *Visual: the big question on screen.*

"Same object. Different speed. How are energy and speed related? Let's investigate."

---

## Production notes
- Keep captions on for accessibility.
- Grade 4 words only (speed, energy, energy of motion, change, faster, slower).
- Do NOT state the full conclusion here — save it for the investigation.
- No kinetic/potential/velocity/mass language.
