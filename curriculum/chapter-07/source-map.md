### Student Reader — Chapter 7 "Solving Problems and Designing Solutions: Thomas A. Edison"
- **Printed pages:** 37–42 · **PDF pages:** 43–48 (canonical Reader; split `Chapter_7_Solving_Problems_Thomas_A_Edison.pdf` = 6 pages). **VERIFIED.**
- **Big Question:** *How did Thomas Edison use his knowledge of energy transfer and transformation to solve problems?* (Reader p.37)
- **Core Vocabulary introduced (Reader page #):** none new — Ch.7 is a Language-of-Instruction review chapter. Supporting/inline terms: filament (p.38 caption) · persist (p.41) · inventor (used, not glossary).
- **Content covered (source-verified):**
  - p.37 — Edison (born Ohio 1847; homeschooled from age 7) designed 1,000+ inventions; used energy transfer + transformation knowledge; carbon filament light bulb.
  - p.38 — "Edison Was a Problem Solver": before light bulbs, open flames (torches/candles/oil lanterns); **Edison did NOT invent the first light bulb** — others had; early bulbs short-lived + expensive; Edison improved designs to be safer/affordable.
  - p.39 — team tested **more than six thousand materials** for filaments; **1879** bamboo filament with carbon coating (electrical → light). Quote: "When you have exhausted all the possibilities, remember this: you haven't."
  - p.40 — "Electrical Energy Where You Need It": **1901** first alkaline battery; wanted lighter/more powerful than lead batteries; electric-car idea didn't work out then (gasoline won); alkaline battery → modern flashlight/remote batteries. Safety note: don't break open batteries (toxic chemicals).
  - p.41 — "Improving Designs Never Ends": quote "I haven't failed. I've just found ten thousand ways that won't work." Persistence defined. **Lewis Howard Latimer** (1848 Chelsea MA; 7 patents; 2 improving the bulb — securing wire to filament, and filament strength/durability; worked for Edison's competitors then Edison's company).
  - p.42 — "Design Solutions over Time": incandescent vs CFL vs LED (less electricity, less heat, longer life); each design improves an earlier one.
