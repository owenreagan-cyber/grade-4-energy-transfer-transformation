# Chapter 4 Teacher Notes

> Background, key distinctions, and pitfalls for teaching "Energy Transfer" (Weeks 4–5).

## Source framing (verbatim intent)
- **energy transfer** = movement of energy from one object to another or from one place to another (Reader p.17; Glossary).
- "All forms of energy—including light, heat, electricity, and sound—are ways that energy transfers, or moves." (Reader p.18)
- "The cup and spoon becoming hot is evidence that energy has transferred (moved)…" (Reader p.20)
- "The glowing light is evidence that energy has moved from one place to another." (Reader p.21)
- **TG L7 "Know the Standards 1":** Transfer = energy moving from place to place or object to object; Transformation = energy changing from one form to another.

## The recurring model (Chapter 4 slice)
```
SOURCE → ENERGY MOVES → TARGET CHANGES (evidence of transfer)
```
Students do NOT observe "energy" — they observe a CHANGE in a target and INFER that energy transferred.

## Transfer vs transformation vs BOTH (teach explicitly — the heart of this chapter)
- **TRANSFER:** energy moves from place to place or object to object.
- **TRANSFORMATION:** energy changes form.
- **BOTH:** energy moves AND changes form (flashlight, buzzer, motor, battery circuit).
- This is the defining distinction of Chapter 4. Do NOT let it collapse. Do NOT force false either/or — BOTH is common and legitimate.
- **Boundary:** "energy transformation" is a Ch.6 glossary term. Use "transformation" as descriptive Grade 4 language (changes form), but do NOT require the formal term or Ch.6 engineering treatment.

## Measured vs. inferred (Chapter 4)
- **MEASURED:** temperature before/after (°C) in the heat investigation.
- **INFERRED:** that energy "moved" / "transferred" — always an inference from an observed change.
- Sound distance is QUALITATIVE (clear / slight / none) unless reliably measured — do NOT fake precision.
- Light path is observed (beam reaches target); transfer is inferred.

## Evidence-first language
- Model: "The paper moved, but nothing touched it." (evidence) → "So sound transferred energy." (claim)
- Always pair a claim with the observed change. Never let students say "I saw energy."

## Safety (locked, non-negotiable)
- **No wall outlets, extension cords, phone chargers, or mains-powered devices** as student experiments.
- **No hot plates or open flames.** Heat = warm water (teacher-controlled, warm not hot) + thermometer.
- **Electricity = low-voltage only** (AA/AAA/D-cell battery holder + LED/bulb/buzzer/motor).
- **No coin cells** by default (if ever: teacher-only, enclosed holder, strict count-in/count-out).
- Sound = cup/membrane + tapping source + paper target. Keep the room still (no bumping desks) so air drafts don't confound the observation.

## Do NOT overteach (Chapter 4 boundary)
- Compression-wave / particle-level sound mechanics.
- Electron flow / circuit equations / current-voltage calculations.
- Conduction / convection / radiation as required vocabulary.
- Kinetic / potential / mechanical / radiant energy terminology.
- Collisions (Ch.5) or engineering design (Ch.6).

## Do NOT
- Claim sound "pushes" through air in a detailed mechanistic way (say: sound energy reaches the paper and causes it to move).
- Claim electricity is "used up" before reaching a device (energy transfers through the circuit).
- Conflate heat and temperature (temperature is a measurement; heat transfer changes it).
- Say students "see energy" (they see a change).

## Vocabulary classification note
- **energy transfer** (p.17) and **sound** (glossary p.28, taught L6) are Core Vocabulary.
- **evidence, claim, reasoning, observation, source, target, transformation** are Language of Instruction / project-preferred terms — model them, don't drill.

## Redesign note (why the stations differ from the TG)

Phase 6 replaces these with flashlight + target (light), warm water + thermometer (heat), and low-voltage battery circuits (electricity).
