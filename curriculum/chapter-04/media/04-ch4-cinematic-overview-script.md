# Cinematic Chapter Overview — Chapter 4: Energy Transfer

> NotebookLM steering prompt: "Produce a calm, cinematic 60–90 second opener for Grade 4 students. Build mystery: 'How can something cause a change somewhere else?' Show four everyday moments — a bell ringing, a flashlight lighting a wall, warm water warming a cup, a battery lighting a bulb. Use gentle music. Use only Grade 4 language. End on the question: 'What evidence shows that energy transferred?' Avoid: compression waves, electron flow, kinetic/potential energy."

## VISUAL / AUDIO SCRIPT

**SHOT 1 — COLD OPEN (0:00–0:08)**
[WIDE, soft focus] A hand taps a small bell on a table. A few feet away, a strip of paper twitches — without being touched.
NARRATOR (warm, curious): "Something just happened… and no one touched it."

**SHOT 2 (0:08–0:20)**
[CLOSE] A flashlight clicks on. The beam lands on a card and lights it up.
NARRATOR: "A light turns on here… and a change appears over there."

**SHOT 3 (0:20–0:34)**
[TABLE] Warm water fills a cup. A metal spoon rests in it. The spoon begins to feel warm.
NARRATOR: "Something warm touches something cool… and the cool thing changes."

**SHOT 4 (0:34–0:48)**
[LOW-VOLTAGE ONLY] A battery snaps into a holder, wires connect, and a small bulb glows.
NARRATOR: "A battery sits here… and a bulb glows way over there."

**SHOT 5 (0:48–1:05)**
[MONTAGE — fast cuts] Bell → paper moves · flashlight → wall lights · warm water → spoon warms · battery → bulb glows. On screen, the model fades in over the montage:
```
SOURCE → ENERGY MOVES → TARGET CHANGES
```
NARRATOR: "In every one of these, energy moved from one place to another. We call that **energy transfer**."

**SHOT 6 (1:05–END)**
[HOLD on the question] "What evidence shows that energy transferred?"
NARRATOR: "This week, you'll look for the evidence. What changed… and how do you know energy moved?"

**TITLE CARD:** Chapter 4 — Energy Transfer

## SAFETY / BOUNDARY NOTES
- Electricity shown is LOW-VOLTAGE battery only. No wall outlets.
- Heat shown is warm (not hot) water. No hot plates or flames.
- No "you can see energy" framing — emphasize the observable change.
