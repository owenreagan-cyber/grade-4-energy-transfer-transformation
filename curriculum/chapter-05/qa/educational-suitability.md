# Educational Suitability — Chapter 5: Collisions Transfer Energy

> Grade 4 appropriateness, accessibility, safety, and engagement.

## Language level
- All student text uses Grade 4 wording: "collide = to come together with impact," "energy of motion," "the target moved." **PASS.**
- No advanced vocabulary (momentum, impulse, kinetic, potential, mechanical, radiant, inertia, deformation, contact force). **PASS.**
- Fill-in and short-answer prompts are short; no paragraph copying. **PASS.**

## Writing / drawing space
- Crash Lab worksheet uses ruled answer lines (≥1–2 lines per prompt) and 6-column data tables with generous row height. **PASS.**
- Notebook inserts include drawing boxes and fill-in blanks, consistent with the drawing-is-core standard. **PASS.**

## Accessibility
- Notebook Insert 3 (lift-the-flap) has a flat, low-motor alternative ("write the answer under the flap"). **PASS.**
- Grayscale-safe (dashed borders, no color-dependent meaning). **PASS.**
- No cutting/folding skill gates mastery — the flap is the only interactive feature, and it has a flat alternative. **PASS.**

## Safety
- Clear crash lane; cars roll (never thrown); Newton's cradle = teacher demo only; no heat measurement; no projectiles. **PASS.**
- No wall outlets, hot plates, open flames, or other hazardous materials. **PASS.**

## Engagement
- Hands-on crash lab (Part A speed + Part B mass) is concrete and low-prep. **PASS.**
- Newton's cradle demo + clap/stomp opening phenomena are age-appropriate hooks. **PASS.**
- Predictions are framed as "a guess we test," supporting the "wrong prediction ≠ failure" norm. **PASS.**

## Cognitive load
- One core idea per slide; fair-test rule repeated as a single phrase ("change ONE thing"). **PASS.**
- Two variables are taught on separate days (Wed speed, Thu mass) to avoid overload. **PASS.**

## Verdict: PASS
- Grade-appropriate language, generous writing space, accessible interactive feature, safe materials, and manageable cognitive load.
