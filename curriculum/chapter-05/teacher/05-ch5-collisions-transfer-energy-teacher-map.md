## Big Questions
- **Chapter:** How is energy involved in collisions?
- **Lesson 11:** What happens when objects collide? How are collisions predictable?

## Essential Understandings
1. A collision happens when two objects come together with impact (make contact).
2. In every collision, changes occur — objects can change speed, direction, or shape, and collisions often produce sound.
3. A moving object has energy of motion; in a collision, some energy of motion transfers to the other object.
4. COLLISION → MOTION CAN CHANGE → ENERGY CAN TRANSFER → ENERGY DOES NOT DISAPPEAR.
5. For the SAME object, faster motion carries greater energy of motion and causes greater change in a collision.
6. For objects moving at about the SAME speed, a heavier object carries greater energy of motion and causes greater change.
7. Collisions are predictable: PREDICT → TEST → OBSERVE → COMPARE → EXPLAIN.

## Student-Friendly Learning Targets
- I can say what a collision is and name changes it can cause (motion, shape, sound).
- I can explain that energy transfers from one object to another during a collision.
- I can compare a SAME car moving faster and slower and tell which causes a greater change.
- I can compare two cars of DIFFERENT weight at about the same speed and tell which causes a greater change.
- I can plan a fair test that changes only ONE thing at a time.
- I can explain that energy does NOT disappear in a collision — it moves or changes form.

## Misconceptions to Address
- "Energy disappears after a collision" (it transfers and partly becomes sound/heat — never gone).
- "A faster car ALWAYS has more energy regardless of object" (compare SAME object at different speeds; do NOT confound mass + speed).
- "The crashing object keeps all its energy" (energy transfers to the target object + some becomes sound).
- "We can see energy" (we observe the CHANGE — motion, shape, sound — and infer transfer).
- "A wrong prediction means we failed" (a mismatch is data; evidence decides).
