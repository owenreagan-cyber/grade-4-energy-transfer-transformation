# Week 6 Daily Lessons — Chapter 5: Collisions Transfer Energy

> Working sequence. Options map to `docs/planning/DAILY_LESSON_ARCHITECTURE.md`. Times assume 40–45 min. Core model: **COLLISION → MOTION CAN CHANGE → ENERGY CAN TRANSFER → ENERGY DOES NOT DISAPPEAR.** Sequence: PREDICT → TEST → OBSERVE → COMPARE → EXPLAIN.

## MONDAY — Collisions Transfer Energy (Option A: concept day)
**Big Question:** How is energy involved in collisions?

1. **Hook / phenomenon (5 min):** Clap your hands; stomp a foot on the floor; tap a pencil on the desk. Ask only: "What changed?" (a sound; a feeling; maybe a shape).
2. **Observe / predict (5 min):** Roll one toy car into a still car. "What do you predict will happen when they crash?"
3. **Notice / wonder (5 min):** "In every collision, something changes. I notice… I wonder…"
4. **Introduce collision (8 min):** **collision = an instance of colliding; collide = to come together with impact.** Model the changes a collision can cause: motion (speed/direction), shape, and sound. Do NOT introduce momentum, impulse, or force calculations.
5. **Read / discuss (12 min):** Reader Ch.5 pp. 25–30. Pause: p.25–26 (motion changes) · p.27 (shape changes) · p.28 (sound) · p.29 (speed) · p.30 (weight).
6. **Notebook cue (5 min):** Insert 1 vocab (collide / collision / sound) + draw the three changes a collision can cause.
7. **Exit (3 min):** "Name two things that can change when objects collide." (motion + shape; + sound)

**Check:** AP 10.1 (Lesson 10 Check) — identify the colliding objects and the energy before/after.

## TUESDAY — Investigating Collisions: Predictions (Option B: lab day, Day 1)
**Big Question:** What happens when objects collide? How are collisions predictable?

1. **Question / prediction (5 min):** "How can we make a fair test of collisions?" Preview the Crash Lab student page.
2. **Fair test (10 min):** WE CHANGE **ONE THING AT A TIME.** Part A changes **ramp height** (same car, same target, same surface). Part B changes **mass/weight** (same ramp, same target, same surface). WE MEASURE **target displacement** (cm). Reinforce: we do NOT measure speed, force, or energy directly — we measure how far the target moves.
3. **Safety / setup (5 min):** clear crash lane; cars ROLL (never thrown); fixed release points; Newton's cradle = teacher demo only.
4. **Predict (15 min):** AP 11.1 — students write their prediction for BOTH Part A and Part B, and name the evidence they will look for (the target moving farther).
5. **Notebook cue (5 min):** Insert 2 — the collision model + "change ONE thing" fair-test rule.
6. **Exit (3 min):** "For Part A, what do we change? What do we keep the same? What do we measure?"

**Core result:** a fair test changes one variable and keeps everything else the same.

## WEDNESDAY — Crash Lab Part A: SPEED (Option B: lab day, Day 2)
**Big Question:** How does the SPEED of the same car affect the change it causes in a collision?

1. **Retrieve predictions (3 min):** "What did we predict for Part A?"
2. **Fair-test check (5 min):** SAME car, SAME target, SAME surface; only ramp height changes (1 / 2 / 3 books). Higher ramp → (inferred) faster car.
3. **Test / record (18 min):** 3 ramp heights × 3 trials each; measure target displacement (cm); record in the data table. Listen for the sound (qualitative — louder/quieter only).
4. **Compare (8 min):** "At which ramp height did the target move farthest?" (highest) "What changed — speed or the car?" (speed).
5. **Explain (6 min):** Higher ramp → faster car → more energy of motion → greater target displacement. Sound is qualitative only.
6. **Exit (3 min):** "What did we change? What did we keep the same? What did we measure?"

**Core result:** same car, faster → greater change (target moves farther).

## THURSDAY — Crash Lab Part B: MASS/WEIGHT + Synthesis + Quiz (Option B→A)
**Big Question:** How does the WEIGHT of a car affect the change it causes — and where does the energy go?

1. **Retrieve (3 min):** "What pattern did Part A show?"
2. **Fair-test check (5 min):** SAME ramp height, SAME target, SAME surface; only mass/weight changes (1 car / 2 cars / 3 cars taped together). Cars roll at about the same speed (same ramp).
3. **Test / record (12 min):** 3 mass conditions × 3 trials each; measure target displacement (cm); listen for the sound (qualitative).
4. **Compare + explain (8 min):** "Which car moved the target farthest?" (heaviest) At about the same speed, a heavier car has more energy of motion.
5. **Synthesis (8 min):** COLLISION → MOTION CAN CHANGE → ENERGY CAN TRANSFER → ENERGY DOES NOT DISAPPEAR. Where did the energy go? To the target (motion) + sound + a tiny bit of heat (not measured).
6. **Notebook cue (4 min):** Insert 3 — misconception flap: "Did the energy disappear?" → flip → "No — it transferred."
7. **Quiz (5–10 min):** Chapter 5 quiz (10 items), or assign as end-of-week check.
8. **Exit (3 min):** "What is one thing that NEVER happens to energy in a collision?" (it never disappears)

**Core result:** same speed, heavier → greater change; energy transfers, never disappears.
