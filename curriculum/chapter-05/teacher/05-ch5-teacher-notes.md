# Chapter 5 Teacher Notes

> Background, key distinctions, and pitfalls for teaching "Collisions Transfer Energy" (Week 6).

## Source framing (verbatim intent)
- **collide, v.** to come together with impact (Reader p.25). **collision, n.** an instance of colliding (p.25).
- **sound, n.** a form of energy that comes from a vibrating object (Glossary p.28; taught L10).
- "A collision happens when two objects make contact." (Reader p.25)
- "The ball strikes the pins, and some of the ball's energy of motion transfers to the pins. This makes the pins gain motion energy and leaves the ball with less motion energy." (Reader p.26)
- "An object moving at faster speed has greater energy of motion than the same object moving at slower speed." (Reader p.29)
- "If two similarly sized objects are moving at the same speed, the one with the greater weight will have more energy of motion." (Reader p.30)

## The recurring model (Chapter 5)
```
COLLISION → MOTION CAN CHANGE → ENERGY CAN TRANSFER → ENERGY DOES NOT DISAPPEAR
```
Students observe a CHANGE (motion, shape, sound) and INFER that energy transferred. They do NOT "see energy."

## The two variables (teach the isolation explicitly)
- **Part A — SPEED:** SAME car, SAME target, SAME surface; only ramp height changes (1/2/3 books). Higher ramp → (inferred) faster car.
- **Part B — MASS/WEIGHT:** SAME ramp, SAME target, SAME surface; only mass/weight changes (1/2/3 cars taped together).
- **NEVER change speed AND mass at once.** Each part isolates one variable. This is the heart of the chapter — do NOT let students confound them.
- **Measured:** target displacement (cm). **Inferred:** relative speed, relative energy of motion, and the energy transfer.

## Sound and heat (locked)
- **Sound:** qualitative only — louder / quieter / about the same. No decibels, no measurements.
- **Heat:** do NOT require measurement. Source-aligned language: "a tiny amount of heat" is produced in a collision but is "difficult to measure." Mention it; do not measure it.

## Evidence-first language
- Model: "The target moved farther." (evidence) → "So the faster car transferred more energy." (claim)
- Always pair a claim with the observed change. Never let students say "I saw energy."

## Safety (locked, non-negotiable)
- Clear crash lane; cars ROLL (never thrown); fixed release points; no launching toward people.
- Newton's cradle = **teacher demonstration only** (phenomenon, not a student lab).
- No heat measurement; no open flames; no projectiles.

## Do NOT overteach (Chapter 5 boundary)
- **momentum · impulse · force calculations · "contact force" · "deformation" as required terms** — all out of scope.
- **kinetic / potential / mechanical / radiant / gravitational potential** — prohibited vocabulary.
- **inertia · compression waves · particle-level sound** — prohibited.
- Engineering design (Ch.6) — deferred.
- Do NOT confound mass + speed in one comparison ("a heavy fast truck vs a light slow marble").

## Do NOT
- Say energy "disappears" or "is used up" in a collision (it transfers and partly becomes sound/heat).
- Say the crashing object "keeps all its energy" (it transfers to the target).
- Require students to state "momentum is conserved" or any conservation equation.
- Let students measure "force" or "impact force."

## Vocabulary classification note
- **collide, collision, sound** are Core Vocabulary.
- **prediction, variable, contact, evidence, transfer, transform** are Language of Instruction / project-preferred terms — model them, don't drill.
- "impact" and "target" and "displacement" are supporting language (teacher-modeled).

## Redesign note (why the Crash Lab differs from the TG)
The official AP 11.2 lets students "change one variable (i.e., height or speed)" in a single open investigation. Phase 7 splits this into two **explicitly isolated parts** — Part A (speed: same car, vary ramp height) and Part B (mass/weight: same ramp, vary mass) — so the two variables are never confounded. The measured outcome is **target displacement** (not clay depth or loudness). This preserves the science (speed and weight each affect collision outcomes) while enforcing the Phase 7 guardrail.
