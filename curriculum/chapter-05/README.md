## Big Questions
- **Chapter:** How is energy involved in collisions?
- **Lesson 11:** What happens when objects collide? How are collisions predictable?

## Core concept
A collision happens when two objects come together with impact. In every collision, changes occur — objects can change speed, direction, or shape, and collisions often produce sound. A moving object has energy of motion; in a collision, some of that energy transfers to the other object. Outcomes are predictable when we isolate ONE variable: speed (Part A) or mass/weight (Part B). Energy does NOT disappear — it moves or changes form.

## Variable isolation (locked)
- **Part A — SPEED:** same car, same target, same surface; vary ramp height only; measure target displacement.
- **Part B — MASS/WEIGHT:** same ramp, same target, same surface; vary mass only; measure target displacement.
- Never confound speed + mass in one comparison.

## Primary ideas (source-verified)
1. A collision is an instance of colliding (two objects come together with impact).
2. Collisions cause changes in motion, shape, and often produce sound.
3. Energy of motion transfers from the moving object to the target in a collision.
4. COLLISION → MOTION CAN CHANGE → ENERGY CAN TRANSFER → ENERGY DOES NOT DISAPPEAR.
5. Faster (same object) → greater energy of motion → greater change.
6. Heavier (same speed) → greater energy of motion → greater change.
7. Collisions are predictable: PREDICT → TEST → OBSERVE → COMPARE → EXPLAIN.
