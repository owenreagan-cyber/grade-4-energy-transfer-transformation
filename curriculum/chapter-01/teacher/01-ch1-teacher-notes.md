# Chapter 1 Teacher Notes

> Background, key distinctions, and common pitfalls for teaching "Energy Causes Change."

## Source framing (verbatim intent)
- **Energy = the ability to cause change** (Reader p.1; TG glossary).
- **Change is evidence of energy** (Reader p.2). Students observe changes to infer energy, because energy itself is not directly visible.
- The unit explicitly does **not** use the terms *kinetic energy* or *potential energy*. Use **energy of motion** and **stored energy**.

## The recurring model (whole unit, introduced here)
```
ENERGY SOURCE → ENERGY FORM → TRANSFER → TRANSFORMATION → OBSERVABLE CHANGE / EVIDENCE
```
For Chapter 1, only the simplest slice is taught: **ENERGY → CHANGE → EVIDENCE**. Do not overteach transfer/transformation yet — the Reader's final Ch.1 section ("Energy Can Move and Change") previews these but says "In later lessons, you will learn more…". Keep that as a preview only.

## Observation vs. inference (teach explicitly)
- **Observed:** "The pencil became shorter and the sharpener made noise."
- **Inferred:** "Electrical energy caused the change."
- Do not let students claim they *saw* energy; they saw the change.

## Stored-energy battery sequence (Thursday)
1. observe battery → 2. predict → 3. place in device → 4. activate → 5. observe change → 6. infer stored energy.
- The loose battery sitting still shows **no visible evidence** of energy. Evidence appears only when the battery is placed in a device and the device changes. This is the exact correction for "only moving objects have energy."

## Misconception watch (from `docs/planning/MISCONCEPTION_MAP.md`)
1. "Only moving objects have energy" → pair every moving example with a stored example (battery, stretched band, raised object).
2. "Energy is a visible substance" → energy is the *ability* to cause change; we see the change.
3. "Stored energy means the object is moving" → stored = ability to cause change *later*.

## Guided reading supports (from TG L1)
- After Reader pp.1–2: "What are other examples of energy causing change?"
- Page 3 (forms of energy): clap three times — "What forms of energy can you observe?" (motion, sound, maybe heat).
- Page 4 (stored energy): "Why do we sometimes have to replace batteries?" (stored chemical energy changes to light and heat; it isn't destroyed).
- Pages 5–6 (stored energy of position): find 3 classroom objects with stored energy of position (book/globe on a shelf).

## Demonstration notes (from TG)
- **Pencil sharpener:** pencil gets shorter/sharper; sharpener goes silent→noisy. Cause: electrical energy (or stored battery chemical energy → electrical). (TG "Know the Science 1" notes the tip may feel warm — treat as optional, teacher-observed.)
- **Flashlight:** motion (thumb on switch) → electrical → light. Review changes in order.
- **Cup+balloon+salt:** sound makes the drumhead vibrate, moving the salt — sound is the cause, salt motion is the effect.
- **Candle (teacher-only):** heat melts wax (cause = heat, effect = melting then re-solidifying).

## Do NOT
- Introduce kinetic/potential/radiant/mechanical/compression-wave/inertia.
- Attach a Performance Expectation to Part A (the source does not).
- Collapse Chapters 4–6 (transfer/transformation) into this introduction.

## Vocabulary classification note
- **evidence** is classified by the TG glossary as Language of Instruction (no Reader page #). We use it heavily because CER depends on it, but never label it "official Core Vocabulary."
