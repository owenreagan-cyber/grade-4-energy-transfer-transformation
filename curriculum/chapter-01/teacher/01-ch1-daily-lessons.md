# Week 1 Daily Lessons — Chapter 1: Energy Causes Change

> Working sequence. Options map to `docs/planning/DAILY_LESSON_ARCHITECTURE.md`. Times assume a 40–45 min period (see classroom simulation for 30-min notes).

## MONDAY — Energy Causes Change (Option A: concept day)
**Primary question:** What changed, and what does that tell us about energy?

**Goals:** introduce energy as the ability to cause change; observe changes; use observable evidence. Do NOT front-load definitions before students observe change.

1. **Hook / phenomenon (5 min):** Flip the classroom light switch off→on. Ask only: "What changed?" Record every change (dark→light, silent→hum). Do not name "energy" yet.
2. **Prediction / noticing (5 min):** Teacher demo — sharpen a pencil with the electric sharpener. Students predict what will change, then watch. Collect: pencil got shorter/sharper, sharpener made noise.
3. **Discussion (8 min):** "Every one of these changes had something making it happen. That something is energy." Introduce: *Energy is the ability to cause change.*
4. **Initial model (7 min):** Build the chain on the board with the class: **ENERGY → CHANGE → EVIDENCE** using the sharpener example.
5. **Reader preview + vocabulary (10 min):** Preview core terms (change, energy, stored energy). Read Reader pp. 1–2 together; pause for "what other changes?" discussion.
6. **Exit (3 min):** "Name one change you noticed today and what form of energy you think caused it."

**AP/source anchor:** L1 opening; AP 1.1 previewed.

## TUESDAY — Energy Detectives / Scavenger Hunt (Option B: lab day)
**Primary question:** Where can we find evidence of energy causing change?

1. **Question / prediction (5 min):** Revisit Big Question. "You are going to be Energy Detectives. What change will you look for first?"
2. **Safety / setup (5 min):** Explain the scavenger hunt; distribute AP 1.1 (ADAPTED student page `01-ch1-energy-detectives-student.docx`).
3. **Investigate (20 min):** Students find **three** classroom examples of change; record Object/System, Before, After, Observable Change, Evidence of Energy, Possible Energy Form. Do NOT pre-fill observations.
4. **Quick debrief (10 min):** Share 2–3 examples; ask "How is that change evidence of energy?"
5. **Exit (5 min):** "Write one question you still have about energy."

**Authoritative foundation:** AP 1.1 (Energy Scavenger Hunt). Source table uses two columns (Something That Changes / How Does It Change?); the ADAPTED page expands to the full evidence model per project standards.

## WEDNESDAY — Forms of Energy (Option C: reading/synthesis day)
**Primary question:** What are some forms of energy?

1. **Retrieve prior evidence (5 min):** Revisit the scavenger-hunt changes from Tuesday.
2. **Demonstration (10 min):** TG L2 demos — flashlight (motion → electrical → light), and the cup+balloon+salt sound demo (sound moves the salt). For each: "What changed? What form of energy was the cause?"
3. **Reader section (10 min):** Read Reader pp. 3–4 (Energy Occurs in Different Forms / carousel). Identify motion, sound, light, heat, electrical energy.
4. **Guided notes (10 min):** Complete the Forms-of-Energy + ENERGY→CHANGE→EVIDENCE sections of the guided notes.
5. **Exit (5 min):** "Name two forms of energy and one change each can cause."

**Avoid** turning this into an isolated vocabulary-definition day — every form is tied to a change/evidence example.

## THURSDAY — Stored Energy + Synthesis (Option C→A)
**Primary question:** Can something have energy even when it is not moving?

1. **Retrieve (5 min):** "Yesterday we saw energy causing changes. Does a battery sitting still have energy?"
2. **Prediction (5 min):** Show a loose battery. Students predict whether it can cause a change. Do NOT claim the loose battery alone visibly proves stored energy.
3. **Battery sequence (10 min):** 1 observe battery → 2 predict → 3 place in flashlight/device → 4 activate device → 5 identify observable change (light) → 6 infer stored energy.
4. **Reader + notes (10 min):** Read Reader pp. 4–5 (Objects Can Store Energy; The Stored Energy of Position). Stretch a rubber band as a second example; raise an object (book/globe) for stored-energy-of-position.
5. **Synthesis (10 min):** Complete the notes' Example/Non-example + Explain It + Exit Reflection. Revisit the week's ENERGY → CHANGE → EVIDENCE model.
6. **Exit (5 min):** "What does stored energy mean? Give one example."
