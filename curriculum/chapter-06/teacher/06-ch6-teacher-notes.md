# CHAPTER 6 TEACHER NOTES — Energy Transformations + Engineering Design

> Background, model, vocabulary boundaries, and safety for teaching Chapter 6 (Weeks 7–8). Read before teaching.

## The core idea
Energy does two things in this unit:
- **TRANSFER** — energy moves from object to object or place to place (the form stays the same).
- **TRANSFORMATION** — energy changes form (one form becomes another).
- **BOTH** — a single system often does both at once.

Chapter 6 introduces **transformation** formally and ties it to **engineering design**: people build devices that transform energy to solve problems.

## Locked model
```
INPUT → CHANGE IN FORM (TRANSFORMATION) → OUTPUT / OBSERVABLE CHANGE
```

## Transfer / Transformation / BOTH — worked examples
| System | Transfer? | Transformation? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm water → cooler spoon | yes (heat moves) | no | TRANSFER |
| Flashlight (battery → bulb) | yes (electrical moves to bulb) | yes (stored → electrical → light + heat) | BOTH |
| Falling dominoes | yes (motion passes down the line) | yes (a little motion → sound) | BOTH |
| Wind-up toy | yes (spring → gears/wheels) | yes (stored → motion) | BOTH |
| Toy-car collision | yes (motion → target) | yes (motion → sound + a little heat) | BOTH |

- Do **not** force false either/or answers. If a device clearly both moves and changes energy, BOTH must remain valid.
- When students get stuck, state the **system boundary** explicitly ("in the battery only…", "in the whole flashlight…").

## Vocabulary boundaries (locked)
**Use:** energy, stored energy, energy of motion, light, heat, sound, electrical energy, energy transfer, energy transformation, convert/conversion, engineering design, criteria, constraints, design, test, refine, predict, observe, evidence.
**Do NOT require:** kinetic energy, potential energy, mechanical energy, radiant energy, chemical energy, joules, power, efficiency, voltage/current calculations, electron flow.
- Source Reader says "stored chemical energy" once (AP 12.1); keep student-facing language as **stored energy** (the glossary term). "Chemical" is teacher-only Language of Instruction.
- Source TG "Know the Science" names "chemical energy" for a flashlight battery; model it to students as **stored energy**.

## Evidence ≠ proof
- The unit standard is **evidence supports an idea**, not "proof." Reword any "prove/proof" phrasing (source AP 13.5 clue 15; supplemental slideshow) to "supports."
- Engineering claims use **supports/evidence**, never "proves."

## Engineering design language
- **Problem** — a want or need to be met.
- **Criteria** — the desired features a successful solution must have (measurable).
- **Constraints** — the limitations (materials, cost, time, safety, size).
- **Design → Build → Test → Refine** — the cycle. **Test 1 failure is data, not a penalty.**
- **Refine** means making a reasoned change based on test evidence (not "make it prettier").

## Capstone (ADAPTED — tabletop signal device)
- Default challenge is a **non-electrical tabletop alert/signal device** (safe, weather-independent). The source's solar oven is an optional EXTENSION only. No wall electricity, no open flame, no hot plate, no projectiles, no real-door hardware.
- Because the device is non-electrical, the expected transformation is typically **stored energy → energy of motion → (sound or visible signal)** via springs/rubber bands/gravity/levers — matching the source's own materials list.

## Safety (locked)
- Tabletop build only; contained footprint.
- No open flame / hot plate / wall electricity / projectile launching / heavy hanging objects near real doors.
- Springs and rubber bands must be handled gently; students stretch, never aim, bands.

## Misconception traps to watch
1. "Transfer and transformation are the same thing" → model the two definitions side-by-side.
2. "A system is only one or the other" → always allow BOTH.
3. "Energy disappears after a transformation" → energy changes form, it does not vanish (sound + a little heat).
4. "Transformation creates new energy" → no, energy changes form.
5. "A failed prototype = bad engineering" → failure is data.
6. "Redesign = decoration" → redesign is an evidence-based change.
7. "Criteria and constraints are the same" → criteria = what we want; constraints = what limits us.
8. "Heaviest/most materials = best" → judge against criteria + evidence, not size.
9. "Energy must be electrical to be useful" → stored/motion/sound/light transformations all solve problems.
