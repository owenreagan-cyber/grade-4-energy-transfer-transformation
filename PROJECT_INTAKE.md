## 5. Instructional principles (established)

1. Preserve the CKSci Student Reader and Teacher Guide as **primary content authorities**.
2. We may expand/enhance/adapt/replace weak activities, but **label provenance clearly** with tags: `SOURCE`, `ADAPTED`, `CUSTOM`, `EXTENSION`.
3. Preferred science rhythm:
   **phenomenon → prediction → investigation/observation → evidence/data → discussion → reading/notes → explanation/application**.
   Avoid defaulting to: slides → copy notes → worksheet.
4. Recurring conceptual question: **"What changed, and what does that change tell us about energy?"**
5. Conceptual distinction:
   - **ENERGY TRANSFER** = energy moves from object to object or place to place.
   - **ENERGY TRANSFORMATION** = energy changes form.
   - Both can occur in the same system.
6. **Grade-4 aligned terminology.** Prefer: stored energy, energy of motion, energy transfer, energy transformation, sound, light, heat, electricity, collision, convert.
   Avoid making these *required core terms* (use only as enrichment/teacher language): kinetic energy, potential energy, gravitational potential energy, mechanical energy, compression wave, radiant energy, inertia. The Teacher Guide explicitly avoids kinetic/potential terminology at this level.
7. For speed and energy: compare the **SAME object at different speeds** when teaching/assessing the core relationship.
8. Fair-test thinking: change one thing · keep important conditions the same · observe/measure the result. Do not overload Grade 4 with advanced variable terminology.
9. Distinguish **OBSERVATION** from **INFERENCE**. Example — Observed: cup moved 22 cm. Inferred: the faster ball had greater energy of motion.
10. Labs should be: safe, low-prep where possible, low-cost, reusable-material heavy, manageable with limited teacher prep, realistic for a private-school budget, supply-orderable early.
11. Teacher prep period is at the **END of the day** → favor reusable kits/bins, short setup, short reset, limited consumables, minimal morning prep.
12. Multi-day labs are acceptable and sometimes preferable.
13. Friday activities must be flexible and **nonessential** to Monday continuation.
14. Videos must have an instructional purpose. (Do NOT research videos in Phase 0.)
15. Future presentation production may use NotebookLM/Gemini, Lovable, Cursor, etc. — but **curriculum decisions are made in this project first**. Do not outsource curriculum logic to presentation-generation tools.

## 6. Lab direction (current 8-lab backbone — planning candidate)

1. Energy Detectives / Energy Scavenger Hunt
2. Moving Marbles — motion energy causes change
3. Ramp It Up! 2.0 — speed and energy evidence
4. Drop It! — height, speed, impact
5. Sound Across Space — evidence of sound transfer
6. Energy Transfer Stations — light, heat, electricity
7. Crash Lab — predicting/testing collisions
8. Energy Engineer — design, test, improve

### Current review findings (recorded, not yet redesigned)

- **Lab 1** — strong concept; battery must produce *observable evidence* (not merely be inspected); avoid claiming unobserved pencil-sharpener heat.
- **Lab 2** — current draft **confounds speed and mass**; core version should use identical marbles; speed belongs primarily in Lab 3; mass comparison may be revisited later.
- **Lab 3** — strong; students measure ramp height and cup displacement; do NOT claim they directly measured speed or energy; graph should be **Ramp Height vs. Cup Displacement**.
- **Lab 4** — use fixed heights (not knee/waist); simplify crater measurement; prefer diameter in cm over difficult mm depth probes.
- **Lab 5** — strong phenomenon; keep focus on evidence of *sound transfer*; do not turn it into a detailed compression-wave unit.
- **Lab 6** — current hot-plate + surge-protector/tablet version is NOT final; redesign toward safer low-prep stations: light (flashlight/target), heat (warm water/cooler object/thermometer), electricity (low-voltage battery circuit + bulb/buzzer/motor).
- **Lab 7** — current version **confounds speed and mass**; isolate variables; target displacement preferable to noisy clay-depth/loudness for core data; Newton's cradle can remain a teacher demonstration.
- **Lab 8** — strong capstone; needs a real multi-page workbook; measurable design criteria; avoid weather-dependent solar task as sole pathway; engineering assessment should emphasize **test → evidence → redesign → retest → explanation**.

## 7. Assessment direction (current custom unit-test PDF is a DRAFT)

Preserve useful ideas: written conceptual assessment · application scenarios · transfer vs transformation · data interpretation · CER · engineering performance assessment · analytic rubric.

Recorded review findings:
- reduce rote vocabulary weighting
- use source-aligned Grade 4 wording
- assess the SAME object at different speeds
- add actual data/graph interpretation
- allow "BOTH" when an example contains transfer and transformation
- avoid advanced kinetic/potential-wave terminology
- do not conflate mass and speed in the core speed standard
- correct source/AP references
- separate student test from teacher key
- strongly consider: **Week 8 Engineering Capstone = performance assessment** and **Week 9 Written Test = separate summative assessment** (rather than a second engineering build during the final test)

The proposed "Pendulum Bell Alert" is a useful **teacher exemplar / post-task model**, not something to show students before they independently design the same task. Its language should use stored energy, energy of motion, energy transfer, energy transformation (not formal potential/kinetic).
